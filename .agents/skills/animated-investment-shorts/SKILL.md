# Animated Investment Shorts

Make a stranger stop, understand one non-obvious idea, and want the report. The output is a story with motion—not a compressed report or animated dashboard.

## Earn the first three seconds

Write three plain-language hooks before building. A hook passes only if a lay viewer can repeat it after one listen.

Reject a story when its hook needs jargon, methodology, several periods, or a new metric. Answer the subject the user requested before pursuing a clever angle.

## Keep one causal spine

Use four to six scenes. Each scene must make the next one necessary. Introduce at most one new concept per scene and one derived metric in the whole cut.

If the story needs repeated caveats, several denominator definitions, or facts from unrelated periods, simplify it or move it to the report. Do not freeze the story until rough keyframes and a spoken cold read work.

## Budget attention, not pixels

Each scene gets:

- a title of at most six words;
- one focal fact or visual comparison;
- one short takeaway only when the visual cannot supply it.

A single comparison may use up to three numbers. Do not repeat a number or conclusion in the title, diagram, and verdict. Remove content before reducing type.

Keep a caveat on-screen only when omitting it would materially reverse the claim. Read [evidence-safety.md](references/evidence-safety.md) before using externally sourced facts or assets.

## Let motion explain the idea

Use one dominant visual action per scene: split, flow, reveal, scale, compression, collision, or transformation. Labels stay attached to what they describe.

The important change must be understandable muted. Avoid card grids, ornamental particles, repeated counters, and movement that merely decorates text. Use one clean transition grammar throughout the cut. Motion must settle.

## Prototype before polishing

Build rough keyframes for two visual directions and compare them beside the strongest existing cut. Reject a direction that is less immediate, less elegant, or less legible.

One integrator edits the active deck. Other agents review the full experience, not isolated compliance checklists.

## Review the viewer's experience

Watch uninterrupted autoplay at 540×960 and 360×640 at normal speed. Use three independent reviews:

1. Hook and story: would someone continue, and can they retell the point?
2. Visual and motion: does the animation teach, impress, and remain readable?
3. Evidence: are the few displayed claims accurate and fairly framed?

Screenshots and bounds diagnose defects; they never certify quality. Compare the final cut side by side with the best prior cut. If it is weaker, it fails regardless of technical passes. Read [realtime-review.md](references/realtime-review.md) for the final review.

## Hand off honestly

Keep working files under `.local/video-production/`. Use the validator only as a linter. Call the result ready for user review—not release-ready—until the actual speaker rehearses it and the user accepts it.
