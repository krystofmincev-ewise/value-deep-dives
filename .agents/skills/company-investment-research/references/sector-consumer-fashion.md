# Consumer, fashion, luxury, retail, and brands playbook

## Map the demand and channel system

Separate brand, product/category, geography, store, e-commerce, marketplace, wholesale, licensing, and franchise economics. Identify who owns inventory, pricing, customer acquisition, returns, discounts, and last-mile cost.

## Operating drivers

- Comparable sales definition, traffic, conversion, transactions, units per transaction, average unit retail, price, promotion/markdown, mix, returns, and foreign exchange.
- Units, sell-in versus sell-through, wholesale orders/cancellations, channel inventory, replenishment, lead times, product freshness, stock-outs, and aged inventory.
- Gross margin bridge through price, mix, freight, sourcing, duties, markdowns, shrink, returns, and channel.
- Store cohort sales, four-wall contribution, occupancy, labor, build cost, payback, closures, impairments, and cannibalization.
- Digital acquisition, repeat, cohort behavior, loyalty, fulfillment/returns, marketplace fees, and contribution margin.
- Brand reach and desirability evidence distinguished from paid impressions, influencer activity, resale noise, or management anecdotes.

## Evidence and sources

Prioritize filings, store/segment notes, inventory and margin disclosures, supplier/retailer evidence, trademark/product records, and reproducible public category data. Census retail/MARTS archives, Census trade, and BLS price data provide category context with release vintages.

Company-level sell-through, traffic, card spend, app/web use, returns, and brand tracking are usually licensed-data gaps. Keep licensed data local and name the unavailable evidence rather than scraping or inferring precision.

## Accounting and thesis traps

- Comparable-sales definitions can exclude closures, currencies, channels, or acquired stores.
- Wholesale shipments can temporarily diverge from consumer demand.
- Inventory value can hide aging, returns, markdowns, and supplier commitments.
- Lease-adjusted leverage and fixed occupancy matter in downside cases.
- Licensing raises margins but changes control, data, and brand risk.
- Growth bought through promotions or paid acquisition may destroy incremental value.
- A viral product or social signal is not a durable brand without cohorts, repeat, distribution, and economics.

## Valuation and catalysts

Use DCF and normalized earnings with store/unit or customer-cohort economics. Adjust peers for channel, brand maturity, geography, leases, cyclicality, reinvestment, and capital returns.

Catalysts include earnings/guidance, holiday or category releases, wholesale order cycles, product launches, pricing, management changes, inventory normalization, store plans, tariffs/trade rules, and celebrity/design events. Pre-register how each event maps to units, margin, cash, and horizon.
