# Energy, utilities, mining, and materials playbook

## Classify exposure

Map every material asset, basin, plant, mine, contract, jurisdiction, working interest, royalty, joint venture, and transport constraint. Separate commodity price exposure from basis, quality, currency, hedge, operating, and fiscal exposure.

## Operating drivers

### Upstream and mining

- Production by commodity, grade and region; decline curves; reserve category and life; recovery assumptions; replacement and finding/development cost.
- Realized price bridge, basis differentials, royalties, taxes, transport, hedges, lifting cost, sustaining capex, abandonment/reclamation, and partner funding.
- Project schedule, remaining capex, contingency, permitting, infrastructure, ramp, recoveries, and country/fiscal risk.

### Midstream, refining, and chemicals

- Contract type, minimum-volume/take-or-pay protection, counterparty credit, throughput, utilization, tariff escalation, recontracting, and maintenance.
- Crack/spread exposure, feedstock, turnaround, yield, inventory accounting, environmental liabilities, and cycle-normalized margins.

### Power, utilities, and renewables

- Rate base, allowed and earned return, regulatory lag, capex plan, load growth, fuel/purchased power, capacity factor, outages, interconnection, curtailment, tax credits, PPAs, merchant tail, and decommissioning.
- Project debt, tax equity, parent guarantees, refinancing, construction milestones, degradation, resource variability, and offtaker quality.

## Evidence and sources

Prioritize filings, reserve reports, regulator dockets/orders, permits, system operators, contract disclosures, and official agency data. Use PUDL as a versioned integration layer for EIA/FERC/EPA/PHMSA/NREL, then verify material claims at the agency. Record release vintage and revision.

## Accounting and thesis traps

- Do not value peak commodity prices or margins as a perpetuity.
- Reconcile successful-efforts/full-cost accounting, depletion, impairments, stripping, inventory methods, derivatives, asset retirement obligations, and reclamation.
- Separate maintenance from growth capex and corporate from project debt.
- Avoid reserve double counting across company disclosures, JVs, and royalty interests.
- Model dilution and financing before project cash flow, not only steady-state NAV.
- Weather, outages, basis, policy, and commodity prices can confound event reactions.

## Valuation and catalysts

Use asset NAV and scenario cash flow with transparent price decks, costs, taxes, timing, and discount rates. Cross-check through-cycle multiples and balance-sheet survival. Utilities require rate-base/allowed-return and funding analysis; contracted infrastructure requires counterparty and terminal/recontracting analysis.

Catalysts include production/project milestones, regulator decisions, permits, contract awards, reserve updates, outages, hedges, capital returns, refinancings, and policy or commodity releases. Bridge each event to the company's actual economic exposure.
