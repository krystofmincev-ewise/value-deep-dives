# Financial institutions playbook

## Resolve the regulated entity

Map listed holding company, operating banks/insurers/managers, regulator identifiers, capital perimeter, ownership, and upstreaming restrictions. For US banks distinguish ticker, CIK, RSSD ID, FDIC certificate, charter, and subsidiary.

## Banks and lenders

- Loan growth/mix, yields, funding mix/cost, deposit beta, non-interest-bearing deposits, brokered/wholesale funding, liquidity, securities duration, unrealized losses, and contingent liquidity.
- Net interest income/margin with rate sensitivity; fee sources; operating efficiency; tangible book value and ROTCE.
- Delinquencies, non-performing assets, criticized/classified loans, net charge-offs, allowance/coverage, CECL assumptions, concentrations, vintages, collateral, and underwriting changes.
- CET1 and other capital ratios, stress losses, risk-weighted assets, payout capacity, and regulatory constraints.

Use FDIC and FFIEC regulatory data plus Federal Reserve/OCC sources where relevant. Record publication availability, not only report period. Use ALFRED vintages for macro features.

## Insurers

- Premium growth/retention, pricing, loss and expense ratios, combined ratio, catastrophe load, reserve development, reinsurance structure/credit, investment yield/duration, surrender behavior, and distribution.
- Statutory capital, RBC/solvency, holding-company liquidity, dividend capacity, asset-liability matching, and embedded guarantees.
- Separate current accident-year profitability from prior-year development and assumption changes.

## Asset managers, exchanges, and specialty finance

- AUM by asset class, market versus net-flow effects, fee rate, performance fees, capacity, concentration, distribution, seed capital, and compensation.
- For exchanges: volume/mix, market data, clearing exposure, open interest, pricing, regulation, and technology uptime.
- For lenders: funding duration, securitization/warehouse terms, gain-on-sale assumptions, residual risk, servicing, and cohort credit performance.

## Accounting and thesis traps

- Price-to-book is not cheap when book value is impaired or returns remain below cost of equity.
- Net interest margin without duration, deposit behavior, credit, and capital is incomplete.
- Regulatory accounting, GAAP/IFRS, and economic value can diverge.
- Loan sales, securitizations, fair-value marks, reserve releases, reinsurance, and one-off performance fees can front-load earnings.
- A listed holding company may not freely access subsidiary capital.

## Valuation and catalysts

Use excess-return/residual-income models and P/TBV versus sustainable ROTCE for banks; normalized underwriting/investment earnings and book value for insurers; and fee-related earnings or cash flow for asset managers. Include capital requirements and dilution.

Catalysts include earnings, regulatory stress tests, rate decisions, credit deterioration, capital actions, reserve reviews, M&A, fund flows, and regulation. Separate event impact on earnings, tangible book, capital, funding, and franchise value.
