# Snap financials, capital structure, and preliminary valuation

**Fact cutoff:** 2026-08-20
**Scope:** financial statements, operating key performance indicators (KPIs), capital structure, restructuring, near-term guidance, and a preliminary 12-month valuation.
**Conventions:** dollars are millions of United States dollars (USD) except per-share data; users and shares are millions. **Fact** means issuer- or United States Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)-reported. **Calculation** means arithmetic from cited facts. **Estimate** means an analyst assumption, not company guidance. Totals may not add because of rounding.

> **Reader key:** This memo defines financial shorthand at first use. The full [glossary and formula guide](../GLOSSARY.md) is available for quick reference. In this memo, **OCF** always means operating cash flow; **CFO** is reserved for the chief financial officer role.

## Executive read-through

Snap's financial trajectory has improved materially, but the quality of that improvement is mixed.

- **Fact:** Revenue increased from $4.61 billion in 2023 to $5.93 billion in 2025, and Q2 2026 revenue grew 19% year over year. Trailing-twelve-month (TTM) revenue through Q2 2026 was $6.35 billion. Adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (adjusted EBITDA) rose from $162 million in 2023 to $689 million in 2025 and $1.02 billion TTM. [2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000013/snap-20251231.htm), [Q2 2026 earnings release](https://s25.q4cdn.com/442043304/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/Q2-26-Earnings-Release_Final.pdf)
- **Fact:** The monetization mix is changing quickly. Advertising grew only 6% in 2025, while "other revenue"—principally subscriptions and partnerships—grew 63% to $745 million. In Q2 2026, advertising revenue grew 9% to $1.28 billion and other revenue grew 85% to $316 million. [2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000013/snap-20251231.htm), [Q2 2026 prepared remarks](https://s25.q4cdn.com/442043304/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/Q2-2026-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)
- **Fact:** High-value geography user trends remain the central weakness. Q2 2026 North America daily active users (DAU) fell 7% year over year to 92 million and Europe DAU fell 2% to 98 million; Rest of World DAU rose 12% to 303 million. The offset was average revenue per user (ARPU): North America +23%, Europe +36%, and Rest of World +4%. This is monetization-led growth, not a broad-based user resurgence.
- **Fact:** Headline cash generation is real: TTM operating cash flow (OCF) was $919 million and issuer-defined free cash flow (FCF) was $706 million. But TTM stock-based compensation (SBC) was $1.03 billion, or 146% of FCF. Treating all SBC as an economic cost produces negative $325 million of "FCF less SBC." That is intentionally conservative, but it shows why unadjusted FCF is not owner earnings.
- **Fact:** Snap's debt management extended maturities and reduced near-term principal risk, but the claim that the current debt is uniformly cheap is no longer correct. In 2025 Snap replaced more than $2 billion of 0%-0.75% converts with $2.05 billion of 6.875% senior notes due 2033/2034. Contractual note interest increased from $7 million in 2024 to $111 million in 2025. [2025 Form 10-K, Note 7](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000013/snap-20251231.htm)
- **Fact:** The April 2026 restructuring affects about 1,000 personnel, including 16% of full-time employees—not 20%—and closes more than 300 open roles. Management targets more than $500 million of annualized cost-base savings by the second half (H2) of 2026. This is an annualized run-rate estimate, not $500 million of 2026 cash savings. [April 2026 Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000119312526155861/d36756d8k.htm), [investor update](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000119312526155861/d36756dex992.htm)
- **Calculation:** At the 2026-08-20 price of $5.21, using Snap's Q2 fully diluted (FD) proxy of 1.881 billion shares and $875 million of net debt, equity value is about $9.80 billion and enterprise value (EV) about $10.67 billion: 1.68x TTM revenue, 10.4x TTM adjusted EBITDA, and 13.9x headline TTM FCF. These apparently low multiples must be read beside negative Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) earnings, rising award overhang, and large cloud commitments.
- **Estimate:** A preliminary 12-month bear/base/bull range is **$2.87 / $6.08 / $10.21** per share. A provisional 30%/50%/20% weighting yields **$5.94**, only 14% above $5.21. The dispersion is the conclusion: upside requires sustained low-teens growth, cost discipline, and containment of dilution; the bear case does not require collapse.

### What the annual history says

1. **Fact:** Snap has not yet produced GAAP operating profit for a full year. The 2025 operating loss narrowed to $532 million, but adjusted EBITDA added back $1.02 billion of SBC, among other items. GAAP and non-GAAP answers are therefore economically far apart.
2. **Calculation:** 2025 FCF margin was 7.4%, up from 0.8% in 2023. Yet 2025 SBC was 17.1% of revenue and 2.3x FCF. SBC fell 23% from its 2022 peak but remains the dominant reconciliation item.
3. **Fact:** Average shares increased 8.7% from 2021 to 2025 despite repeated buybacks. Buybacks have largely offset issuance rather than created meaningful per-share contraction.
4. **Fact:** Advertising was $5.19 billion, or 87% of 2025 revenue, down from 96% in 2023. Advertising revenue grew $282 million in 2025; other revenue grew $288 million from a much smaller base. The diversification thesis is now observable, not merely prospective.
5. **Fact:** In 2025 ad impressions increased about 17%, while cost per impression declined about 10%. That combination produced only 6% advertising revenue growth. More inventory is not automatically more valuable inventory; price/yield remains a key proof point. [2025 Form 10-K, MD&A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000013/snap-20251231.htm)

## Cash flow quality, SBC, and dilution

### Headline FCF versus owner economics

- **Fact:** TTM Q2 2026 FCF was $706 million, up sharply from $219 million in FY2024. Snap had delivered positive FCF in eight consecutive quarters by Q2 2026. [Q2 2026 prepared remarks](https://s25.q4cdn.com/442043304/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/Q2-2026-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)
- **Fact:** TTM SBC was $1.031 billion. Because SBC is added back in OCF, headline FCF does not charge the current-period income-statement expense. A full "FCF minus SBC" adjustment is conservative—it assumes grant-date expense is the right cash-equivalent cost—but ignoring SBC entirely is more misleading for Snap.
- **Calculation:** TTM FCF less SBC was negative $325 million. FCF covered only 68% of SBC.
- **Fact:** Most physical capex relates to leased-office improvements. Core computing is largely purchased as cloud/hosting opex. Therefore, Snap's low $214 million TTM capex is not evidence of a low-reinvestment business. [2025 Form 10-K, liquidity and commitments](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000013/snap-20251231.htm)

Capital-structure assessment:

1. **Fact:** All notes rank equally and are unsecured/unsubordinated. Convertible notes may settle in cash, shares, or a mix at Snap's election. On a fundamental change, holders can put converts at par plus accrued interest. Senior notes contain customary covenants/default provisions and a 101% change-of-control put.
2. **Fact:** Remaining capped calls were out of the money at 2025 year-end. Their cap prices were $32.58, $121.02, $93.90, and $33.48 for the 2026/2027/2028/2030 notes. At $5.21, current convertible dilution is remote; stock awards are the immediate dilution issue.
3. **Fact:** In 2025 Snap repurchased more than $2 billion of low-coupon 2027/2028 converts and financed that duration extension principally with $2.05 billion of 6.875% senior notes. It also booked debt-extinguishment gains by buying some converts below carrying value. This was capable treasury execution in maturity-risk terms, but it raised recurring cash interest.
4. **Fact:** Contractual note interest was $111 million in 2025 versus $7 million in 2024; total note interest expense was $117 million versus $16 million. The statement "Snap secured cheap debt" is historically true for the converts and false for the present senior-note layer.
5. **Fact:** Snap has a $1.05 billion senior unsecured revolver: $250 million expires May 2027 and $800 million expires February 2030. It was undrawn at 2025 year-end, with $82 million of letters of credit. The undrawn fee is 0.10%; dollar loans price at the Secured Overnight Financing Rate (SOFR) + 0.85% or the base rate. The facility's minimum unrestricted cash and cash-equivalent covenant was increased from $300 million to $800 million. At Q2's $959 million cash balance, the cash-only cushion was about $159 million; marketable securities should not be assumed to satisfy the covenant without the agreement definition.

## Restructuring and cost discipline

**Facts** from Snap's [April 2026 Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000119312526155861/d36756d8k.htm), [employee memo](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000119312526155861/d36756dex991.htm), and [investor update](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000119312526155861/d36756dex992.htm):

- Approximately 1,000 total personnel affected, including 16% of full-time employees; more than 300 open positions closed.
- $95-$130 million of estimated pre-tax charges and $75-$100 million of estimated future cash expenditures, mostly in Q2 2026.
- More than $500 million of annualized cost-base savings expected by H2 2026.
- Full-time employees fell from 5,381 at Q1 2026 to 4,723 at Q2, a 12.2% sequential reduction. The difference from 16% reflects timing/scope and the inclusion of non-employee personnel in the approximately 1,000 figure.

**Interpretation:** Q2's $129 million restructuring charge obscures the run-rate improvement. Management said personnel savings should be more fully reflected from Q3. The correct catalyst is therefore not "Q2 beat means Q3 must beat"; it is whether the Q3 cost base and revenue guidance convert into EBITDA/FCF without rebuilding headcount or shifting the spend into infrastructure and Specs.

### What the price implies

At $10.67 billion EV:

- **Calculation:** A 1.5x forward EV/revenue multiple requires $7.12 billion of next-twelve-month revenue, 12.0% above TTM.
- **Calculation:** A 1.7x multiple requires $6.28 billion, roughly flat to TTM.
- **Calculation:** A 2.0x multiple requires $5.34 billion, 16% below TTM.
- **Calculation:** At 12x equity/FCF, the current equity value requires $817 million of forward FCF; at 15x, it requires $653 million. Snap already produced $706 million TTM, but that cash-flow measure excludes the economic cost of $1.03 billion TTM SBC.

The market is not pricing a revenue collapse if Snap deserves only 1.5x EV/revenue; it is pricing a combination of modest growth, low-quality cash conversion, dilution, governance/strategy risk, and uncertain terminal margins. The rerating thesis must improve at least two of those, not merely show DAU growth.

## Bottom line for the integrated report

Snap is no longer the same financial object it was in 2022-2023: revenue growth has recovered, direct revenue is a real second engine, adjusted costs are showing leverage, and FCF is positive. A concentrated position cannot be justified by "cheap users" alone. The most material counterweights are (1) shrinking North America/Europe DAU, (2) SBC above FCF, (3) award overhang rising despite buybacks, (4) $3.4 billion of cloud/other commitments, and (5) the expensive 6.875% layer added to the debt stack.

At $5.21 the stock is not obviously expensive, but it is not an effortless 2x either. The base case supports roughly $6.08 in 12 months; reaching $10 requires Snap to prove that monetization, subscription growth, gross-margin expansion, and cost discipline can coexist without renewed dilution or Specs-driven spending. The primary financial catalyst is Q3 execution against already-raised expectations, not merely the appearance of restructuring savings.
