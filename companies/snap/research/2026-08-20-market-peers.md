# Snap market, audience, and peer economics

**Evidence cutoff:** 2026-08-20
**Purpose:** market-size, audience, competitive-share, and peer-economics module for the Snap investment deep dive
**Method:** public primary filings and industry-association data are preferred. Survey evidence is used for demographics; advertising reach, registered accounts, active users, and estimated people are not treated as interchangeable.

> **Reader key:** User, advertising, market, and artificial-intelligence (AI) abbreviations are expanded at first substantive use. See the full [glossary and formula guide](../GLOSSARY.md).

## Executive conclusion

Snap operates in a very large and still-growing market, but its latest results do not show broad share capture. Dentsu's May 2026 forecast puts worldwide advertising spend at $1.06 trillion in 2026, with digital representing 69%, implying roughly $731 billion of digital spend. Dentsu expects total advertising to grow 5.0% in 2026; its December forecast put digital growth at 6.7% and social growth at 11.4%. [Dentsu mid-year global forecast, 2026-05-27](https://www.dentsu.com/news-releases/ad-spend-growth-is-projected-to-slow-to-5-percent-in-2026-still-outpacing-economic-growth) [Dentsu December forecast, 2025-12-03](https://www.dentsu.com/news-releases/global-ad-spend-set-to-surpass-one-trillion-for-the-first-time-in-2026-as-the-algorithmic-era-redefines-growth)

Snap's Q2 2026 advertising revenue grew 9% to approximately $1.28 billion. That was better than Q1's 3%, but slower than Meta advertising at 27%, YouTube advertising at 13%, Pinterest total revenue at 18%, and Reddit advertising at 64%. [Snap Q2 2026 prepared remarks, 2026-08-03](https://s25.q4cdn.com/442043304/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/Q2-2026-Prepared-Remarks.pdf) [Meta Q2 2026 results, 2026-07-29](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx) [Alphabet Q2 2026 results, 2026-07-22](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000066/googexhibit991q22026.htm) [Pinterest Q2 2026 results, 2026-08-04](https://s204.q4cdn.com/369458543/files/doc_earnings/2026/q2/earnings-result/Q2-2026-Press-Release.pdf) [Reddit Q2 2026 results, 2026-07-30](https://investor.redditinc.com/news-events/news-releases/news-details/2026/Reddit-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)

The market opportunity is therefore not the dispute. The underwriting question is whether Snap can move from **below-peer growth to above-market growth** while its highest-value regions lose daily users. In Q2 2026, North American daily active users (DAU) fell 7% and European DAU fell 2%, while Rest-of-World DAU grew 12%. Quarterly total average revenue per user (ARPU) was $10.26, $3.62, and $1.00 in those regions, respectively. [Snap Q2 2026 results, 2026-08-03](https://investor.snap.com/news/news-details/2026/Snap-Inc--Announces-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx) The incremental user is worth much less today than the user Snap is losing in North America; monetization gains must outrun that mix shift.

Snap retains a defensible young-audience position. It says it reaches more than 75% of 13-34-year-olds in more than 20 countries, including the United States, and its 2025 Form 10-K states that most users are 18-34. [Evan Spiegel, “14 Years at Snap,” 2025-09-05](https://newsroom.snap.com/14-year-letter?avalonEnableChatWidget=cyj08&lang=en-GB) [Snap 2025 Form 10-K, filed 2026-02-05](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000013/snap-20251231.htm) But U.S. survey evidence shows that young-audience strength is shared: among adults 18-29, 95% used YouTube, 80% Instagram, 63% TikTok, 58% Snapchat, and 48% Reddit in Pew's 2025 survey. Among teens, Snapchat trailed YouTube, TikTok and Instagram in both reach and daily use. [Pew Social Media Fact Sheet, 2025-11-20](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/) [Pew Teens, Social Media and AI Chatbots, 2025-12-09](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/12/09/teens-social-media-and-ai-chatbots-2025/)

My 12-month broad-market share framework is:

> **Share formula:** `indicative Snap share = annualized Snap advertising revenue / broad digital advertising spend`. This uses mismatched time periods and a deliberately broad denominator, so it is directional rather than a reported market share.

| Scenario | Snap ad growth | Broad digital market assumption | Implied movement in Snap's broad digital share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | 2% | 7% | about -5% relative; indicative share falls from ~0.70% to ~0.67% |
| Base | 12% | 7% | about +5% relative; indicative share rises to ~0.74% |
| Bull | 20% | 7% | about +12% relative; indicative share rises to ~0.79% |

The starting share annualizes one quarter of Snap advertising revenue against Dentsu's annual global digital forecast, so it is an order-of-magnitude indicator, not a reported market share. The direction is more useful than the decimal: Snap needs more than roughly 7% advertising growth to gain share of broad digital and more than roughly 11% to gain share of the faster-growing social category.

Competitor regulation is not a dependable upside catalyst. The U.S. TikTok shutdown scenario was removed from the base case when a U.S. joint venture was completed in January 2026. In Australia, the under-16 restriction applies to Snapchat as well as Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Reddit and YouTube. In the EU, restrictions on targeted advertising to minors apply across Snapchat, TikTok and Meta platforms, and Snap itself is a designated very large online platform. The closest substitutes for disrupted TikTok video time are Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts; Snap would likely receive only a secondary benefit while carrying unusually high youth exposure itself.

## The addressable market

### Global: large, but definitions disagree

Ad-market forecasts are not financial statements. They differ on gross versus net revenue, agency commissions, political advertising, retail media, owned-and-operated inventory, geography and currency. Two reputable 2026 forecasts illustrate the range:

- Dentsu forecasts $1.06 trillion of global advertising spend, up 5.0%, with digital at 69% of the market. That implies roughly $731 billion of digital spend. [Dentsu mid-year forecast](https://www.dentsu.com/news-releases/ad-spend-growth-is-projected-to-slow-to-5-percent-in-2026-still-outpacing-economic-growth)
- World Advertising Research Center (WARC) forecasts $1.30 trillion of global advertising spend, up 9.1%, and says almost 80% flows to retail media, paid search and social platforms. [WARC Future of Media 2026, 2026-01-15](https://www.warc.com/en/press/press-releases/26-01-15_systems-planning-a-new-model-for-media-planning-as-traditional-approaches-collapse)

These figures should not be averaged. For Snap, a reasonable valuation prior is a **$700-$800 billion broad global digital pool**, with social and online video growing at high-single to low-double digits. Snap does not need the market to accelerate to grow; it needs to take a small amount of budget from much larger incumbents.

Snap's Q2 2026 advertising revenue of about $1.28 billion annualizes to roughly $5.13 billion, only about 0.70% of the Dentsu-derived broad digital pool. That denominator includes search, retail media and formats Snap does not address, so the calculation understates Snap's share of its narrower social/video market. It is nevertheless useful for disproving a total addressable market (TAM) constraint: even a modest fraction of broad digital budget is material relative to Snap.

### United States and Europe

The Interactive Advertising Bureau (IAB) and PricewaterhouseCoopers (PwC) report that United States internet advertising revenue reached $294.6 billion in 2025, up 13.9%. [IAB/PwC Internet Advertising Revenue Report, 2026-04-16](https://www.iab.com/insights/internet-advertising-revenue-report-full-year-2025/) IAB Europe reports that digital advertising across 30 European markets reached €131.1 billion, up 10.5%; display grew 12.5%, social reached €35.5 billion and grew 19.2%, and video reached approximately €34 billion and grew 19.6%. [IAB Europe AdEx Benchmark 2025, 2026-07-07](https://iabeurope.eu/iab-europes-adex-benchmark-2025-report/) [IAB Europe report page](https://iabeurope.eu/knowledge_hub/iab-europe-adex-benchmark-2025-report/)

Precise Snap shares by geography are not inferable from public filings:

- Snap reports North America, Europe, and Rest of World, which do not align exactly with IAB country sets.
- Snap's regional revenue includes subscriptions and other non-advertising revenue. Other revenue was almost 20% of Q2 2026 total and grew 85%.
- Platform revenue and advertiser spend can differ because of gross/net presentation, rebates, taxes and reseller arrangements.
- Currency and the geographic basis for assigning revenue differ.

For scale only, annualizing Snap's Q2 North American **total** revenue gives $3.77 billion, versus $294.6 billion of U.S.-only digital advertising. The resulting 1.3% ratio is not a market share: its numerator includes Canada and non-ad revenue while its denominator excludes Canada and non-ad formats. Europe presents the same mismatch. This report therefore does not publish a false-precision regional share.

The last clean regional advertising-growth disclosure in the reviewed record was Q3 2025: North American ad revenue grew only 1%, Europe 12%, and Rest of World 13%. North American small- and medium-sized business (SMB) revenue grew 25%, but large-customer-services revenue declined and represented 43% of global revenue. [Snap Q3 2025 investor letter, 2025-11-05](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440825000063/q32025investorletter.htm) This is the clearest evidence that Snap's most important share problem was concentrated in large North American advertisers rather than global reach.

### Japan and other high-value markets

Dentsu's domestic study reports that Japanese internet-media advertising grew 11.8% to ¥3.31 trillion in 2025; social advertising grew 18.7% to ¥1.31 trillion, and 2026 internet-media spend is forecast to grow 8.3%. [Dentsu Japan Internet Advertising Expenditures, 2026-03-05](https://www.dentsu.co.jp/en/news/release/2026/0305-011007.html) Snap does not disclose Japan revenue or DAU, so share is not inferable. The market illustrates a broader point: Rest-of-World is not a single low-value bloc. Japan, Australia and parts of the Gulf have attractive ad economics, while India and many emerging markets have much lower current ARPU. A regional model that applies one Rest-of-World monetization curve will be misleading.

The derived daily-denominator figures make the gap visible but should not be mistaken for standardized ARPU. Meta generated about 6.3 times Snap's advertising revenue per reported daily person/user in Q2; Reddit generated about 2.25 times Snap's. Meta's denominator includes WhatsApp users who may see few or no ads, while Snap's registered-user definition is stricter than Reddit's logged-out unique definition. The direction—substantial Snap monetization headroom—is credible; the exact multiple is not.

Pinterest demonstrates the opposite denominator trap. Its $1.86 quarterly ARPU is below Snap's $3.25, but Pinterest divides by monthly active users and Snap by daily active users. That does not mean a Snap monthly user is worth more. YouTube and TikTok do not disclose the denominator needed for the comparison.

### Revealed advertiser allocation

The most reproducible advertiser-sentiment measure is actual platform revenue growth. It captures budgets after sales pitches, case studies and product launches:

| Platform | Q2 2026 ad or closest revenue growth | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Reddit ads | +64% | Rapid share gain from a small base; international revenue +84%. |
| Meta ads | +27% | Share gain at enormous scale; impressions +14% and price per ad +12%. |
| Pinterest total | +18% | Healthy growth with MAU +11% and ARPU +7%; total revenue is shown because the release does not split advertising. |
| YouTube ads | +13% | Above broad digital-market growth; direct response and major-event video remain strong. |
| Snap ads | +9% | Improving, but below the disclosed peer group and close to/below social-market growth. |

For an additional directional check, the five disclosed Q2 revenue lines—Meta ads, YouTube ads, Snap ads, Pinterest total, and Reddit ads—formed a $73.6 billion quarterly pool in Q2 2026. Snap represented about 1.74%, down from approximately 1.99% in Q2 2025. This is **not industry market share**: it omits TikTok and many channels, counts Pinterest total revenue, and combines different products. It is a consistent indication that Snap lost share to this disclosed peer set over the year.

Snap's internal evidence is more positive: active advertisers grew 28% in Q4 2025, and North American SMB remained a growth driver. [Snap Q4 2025 investor letter, 2026-02-04](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000011/snapincq42025investorlet.htm) Active-advertiser count can rise while average spend falls, however. Until advertiser retention, cohort spend, or auction density is disclosed, revenue and effective cost per thousand impressions (eCPM) deserve more weight than logo counts.

## Audience and engagement

### Snap's demographic strength is real but concentrated

Snap's 2025 Form 10-K says the majority of its users are 18-34 and explicitly warns that this group may be less brand loyal and more likely to follow viral trends. [Snap 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000013/snap-20251231.htm) Snap also says it reaches more than 75% of 13-34-year-olds in more than 20 countries, including the United States. This is **penetration inside selected countries**, not the percentage of all Snap users who are 13-34 and not a global statistic. [“14 Years at Snap,” 2025-09-05](https://newsroom.snap.com/14-year-letter?avalonEnableChatWidget=cyj08&lang=en-GB)

The attraction for advertisers is a difficult-to-reach messaging audience. The concentration risks are:

- aging users may reduce use when friend groups move or messaging habits change;
- high penetration among young people limits domestic user-growth headroom;
- age-assurance and youth-safety regulation disproportionately touches the core audience;
- young audiences are valuable to brands but may have lower current income and are subject to tighter targeting rules;
- the fastest user growth is occurring in lower-ARPU countries.

There is early evidence of broader age reach: Snap said its U.S. audience broadened sequentially in Q2 2026, led by people 35 and older. [Snap Q2 2026 prepared remarks](https://s25.q4cdn.com/442043304/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/Q2-2026-Prepared-Remarks.pdf) One quarter of directional commentary does not yet establish retention or monetization of older cohorts.

### Independent U.S. survey comparison

Pew surveyed 5,022 U.S. adults from February 5 through June 18, 2025 using address-based probability sampling and web, mail and phone responses. Its “ever use” rates by age were: [Pew Social Media Fact Sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/)

| Platform | 18-29 | 30-49 | 50-64 | 65+ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| YouTube | 95% | 92% | 85% | 64% |
| Facebook | 68% | 80% | 74% | 57% |
| Instagram | 80% | 62% | 40% | 19% |
| TikTok | 63% | 44% | 30% | 12% |
| Reddit | 48% | 35% | 16% | 6% |
| Snapchat | 58% | 31% | 13% | 4% |

Snapchat's 54-point gap between 18-29 and 65+ is the steepest in this table. TikTok and Instagram also skew young, but YouTube is nearly universal and Meta combines a young-skewed Instagram with an older-skewed Facebook. Pinterest was not included in Pew's 2025 age table and is not backfilled with an older survey.

Among 1,458 U.S. teens ages 13-17 surveyed September 25-October 9, 2025, Pew found roughly nine in ten used YouTube, 63% TikTok, 61% Instagram and 55% Snapchat. Daily use was roughly three-quarters for YouTube, 61% TikTok, 55% Instagram and 46% Snapchat. Around one-fifth used YouTube or TikTok almost constantly, versus 12% for Snapchat and Instagram. [Pew Teens, Social Media and AI Chatbots 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/12/09/teens-social-media-and-ai-chatbots-2025/)

This survey does not measure minutes, messages, purchase intent or closeness of relationships. It does show that Snap is important rather than dominant among U.S. teens. The platform's advantage is the nature of use—private visual communication—not the largest reach or highest self-reported frequency.

### Geographic mix is the central monetization tension

| Snap region, Q2 2026 | DAU | YoY | Total revenue | YoY | Official quarterly ARPU | YoY |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| North America | 92m | -7% | $942.9m | +15% | $10.26 | +23% |
| Europe | 98m | -2% | $353.8m | +33% | $3.62 | +36% |
| Rest of World | 303m | +12% | $302.3m | +17% | $1.00 | +4% |

Source: [Snap Q2 2026 results](https://investor.snap.com/news/news-details/2026/Snap-Inc--Announces-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx). Revenue and ARPU include subscriptions and other revenue.

Rest of World was 61.5% of DAU but only 18.9% of quarterly revenue. North America was 18.7% of DAU and 59.0% of revenue. A one-million-DAU loss in North America therefore cannot be offset economically by one million incremental DAU in Rest of World at current monetization. At the reported ARPUs, it takes roughly ten Rest-of-World daily users to equal one North American daily user in quarterly revenue.

The mix is not destiny. Europe ARPU grew 36% while DAU declined, and Rest-of-World revenue grew faster than ARPU because the audience expanded. But subscription growth complicates the interpretation. The valuation model should separate:

1. DAU retention in North America and Europe;
2. advertising yield per DAU by region;
3. paying-subscriber penetration and price;
4. Rest-of-World country mix rather than treating it as homogeneous.

## TikTok and YouTube: the hardest attention competitors

TikTok does not publish a global quarterly user/revenue bridge comparable with Snap. TikTok said 178 million people in the European Union (EU) used the service monthly for Digital Services Act (DSA) purposes in the second half of 2025 and separately said more than 200 million people across its broader European footprint used it monthly. [TikTok sixth DSA transparency report, 2026-04-29](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/digital-services-act-our-sixth-transparency-report-on-content-moderation-in-europe?lang=en-150) [TikTok European community update, 2025-09-05](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/fi-200-m?lang=fi-FI) Those are different geographies, not contradictory figures. ByteDance revenue estimates often combine China-based Douyin, TikTok and other products; they are excluded from the peer table because ByteDance does not publish a reconciled current figure.

YouTube reported $11.06 billion of Q2 2026 advertising revenue, up 13%. Alphabet does not disclose a quarterly active-user denominator, and YouTube subscription revenue sits in a broader Google line. [Alphabet Q2 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000066/googexhibit991q22026.htm) In 2025, YouTube exceeded $60 billion of combined annual advertising and subscription revenue, showing how far video monetization can extend beyond ads. [Alphabet Q4 2025 earnings call, 2026-02-04](https://abc.xyz/investor/events/event-details/2026/2025-Q4-Earnings-Call-2026-Dr_C033hS6/default.aspx)

For Snap, TikTok and YouTube are both attention competitors and creative standards. YouTube's broad age coverage and connected-TV presence are difficult for Snap to match; TikTok's entertainment graph and creator supply are the closest challenge to Spotlight. Snap's more defensible behavior is private communication. This reduces direct substitutability but also creates fewer safe ad slots than an infinite entertainment feed.

## Regulatory share transfer

### TikTok: binary U.S. upside is no longer a base case

TikTok finalized a U.S. joint venture in January 2026, avoiding the then-imminent U.S. shutdown; the service continued in the same app for more than 200 million U.S. users. [Associated Press, 2026-01-23](https://apnews.com/article/eccb46c3bfee4cf3d362a01fe4968a4f) The exact ownership and economics may still evolve, but an outright ban is no longer the appropriate base assumption.

If a future disruption did remove TikTok supply, the likely ordering of beneficiaries is:

1. Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts, because their creator, video, recommendation and advertiser workflows are closest;
2. Snap Spotlight for some young-user time and budgets;
3. Reddit, Pinterest and other platforms for narrower contexts.

That ranking is an inference from product substitutability, not a disclosed forecast. Snap could receive a meaningful percentage gain from a small revenue base while still capturing a small share of the displaced absolute dollars.

### Youth regulation is a common shock, not a Snap hedge

Australia's age restriction has required Facebook, Instagram, Reddit, Snapchat, TikTok, YouTube and several other services to take reasonable steps to prevent under-16 accounts since December 10, 2025. Pinterest is currently outside the age-restricted list. [Australian eSafety Commissioner FAQ, updated 2026-04-01](https://www.esafety.gov.au/about-us/industry-regulation/social-media-age-restrictions/faqs) The regulator's March 2026 update moved Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok and YouTube into focused compliance investigations. [eSafety compliance report, 2026-03-31](https://www.esafety.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-03/SocialMediaMinimumAgeComplianceUpdateMarch2026.pdf?v=1780749369078)

The first three-month evaluation found reported recent account use fell across Snapchat, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook and YouTube among the surveyed population; Snapchat and YouTube both moved from 23.3% at follow-up, TikTok to 21.7%, and Instagram to 19.3%. These figures are survey percentages from an early evaluation, not audited platform DAU. [eSafety early evaluation, 2026-07](https://www.esafety.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-07/Early-days-early-insights-SMMA-evaluation-three-months-July-2026.pdf?v=1786469342003)

In the EU, the Commission preliminarily found in April 2026 that Meta had not adequately prevented under-13 access to Facebook and Instagram; in July it preliminarily found TikTok's minor-account settings insufficient. [European Commission Meta findings, 2026-04-29](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-preliminarily-finds-meta-breach-digital-services-act-failing-prevent-minors-under-13) [European Commission TikTok findings, 2026-07-24](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_1679/IP_26_1679_EN.pdf) However, the DSA's ban on targeted advertising to minors already applies to Snapchat, TikTok, Instagram and Facebook, and Snap is itself a designated very large online platform (VLOP). [European Commission DSA impact summary, accessed 2026-08-20](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/dsa-impact-platforms) [European Commission VLOP supervision list, accessed 2026-08-20](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/list-designated-vlops-and-vloses)

The investment implication is asymmetric:

- a competitor-specific TikTok outage would help Snap at the margin;
- rules aimed at feeds, recommender design, or targeted ads can hurt all platforms;
- age-assurance rules are particularly relevant to Snap because of its demographic concentration;
- Meta can absorb compliance cost and shift demand across Facebook/Instagram more easily;
- Pinterest may be a relative beneficiary where its product remains outside youth-social definitions.

No regulatory windfall is included in the base or bull share scenarios.

## App-store and advertiser sentiment: what is and is not reproducible

No app-store sentiment series is included. Apple and Google display current ratings and selected reviews, but they do not provide a stable public six-month historical panel of ratings, review deletions, country mix, app-version mix and denominator changes. Scraping the latest visible reviews would overweight users motivated to complain or praise and could not establish whether sentiment improved. A reproducible analysis would require dated country-level snapshots from the same provider, review-volume weights, bot/spam controls, language classification and version normalization.

Advertiser sentiment is represented here through reported revenue growth, eCPM, advertiser-cohort evidence and external surveys only where methods are disclosed. Issuer-selected case studies are not market sentiment. The Q2 peer revenue table is the strongest recent revealed-preference evidence: advertisers increased spend faster on Meta, Reddit, Pinterest and YouTube than on Snap.

## Twelve-month share scenarios

The following is a mechanical framework using Q2 2026 Snap advertising revenue annualized ($5.13 billion) and the Dentsu-derived 2026 global digital pool (~$731 billion). It assumes the broad pool grows 7% over the next twelve months. The decimal shares are not claimed as measured industry shares; they provide internally consistent scenario direction.

| Scenario | Snap next-12m ad growth | Snap indicative ad revenue | Broad digital pool | Ending broad-pool ratio | Relative share change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | 2% | $5.23bn | $782.6bn | 0.67% | -4.7% |
| Base | 12% | $5.75bn | $782.6bn | 0.73-0.74% | +4.7% |
| Bull | 20% | $6.16bn | $782.6bn | 0.79% | +12.1% |

### Bear: continued mix and auction pressure

- North American and European DAU remain negative.
- Rest-of-World growth supplies lower-value impressions faster than local demand grows.
- Meta/YouTube/TikTok retain most incremental video and performance budget.
- Snap advertising grows below the market despite stronger subscription revenue.

### Base: modest share recovery

- North American DAU stabilizes near current levels and older cohorts offset some youth churn.
- Advertising growth reaches roughly 10-14%, just above broad digital and around the social-market hurdle.
- Dynamic Product Ads (DPA)/app and Sponsored Snaps absorb supply without a large eCPM decline.
- Europe and selected high-value Rest-of-World countries grow faster than global average.

### Bull: real budget reallocation

- Snap achieves 18-22% advertising growth while eCPM turns flat-to-positive.
- North American large advertisers reaccelerate, not merely SMB account counts.
- Independent measurement confirms incremental reach/return and agencies increase recurring allocations.
- Competitor disruption contributes at most a small tailwind; execution, not regulation, drives the case.

## Falsifiers and monitoring dashboard

The market-share thesis weakens if:

- Snap advertising grows below 7% while broad digital remains high-single-digit;
- Snap remains the slowest-growing disclosed peer for another two quarters;
- North American DAU falls more than mid-single digits without an offsetting increase in ad-only revenue per DAU;
- Rest-of-World DAU grows double digits but ARPU remains roughly flat, showing local advertiser demand is not developing;
- active-advertiser growth does not translate into revenue, eCPM, or cohort-spend growth;
- older U.S. audience growth is not repeated or retained;
- regulation reduces Snap youth engagement at least as much as TikTok/Instagram, eliminating the proposed share-transfer benefit.

The thesis strengthens if:

- ad revenue grows 12-15% for two quarters and eCPM approaches flat while impressions still grow;
- North American large-customer revenue and SMB revenue grow simultaneously;
- North American DAU stabilizes and European DAU returns to growth;
- Europe and high-value Rest-of-World markets produce ad-only growth above local digital-market rates;
- external measured ROI causes disclosed budget reallocation rather than one-off tests;
- Snap's share of the disclosed peer-revenue pool stops declining.

## Bottom line for valuation

Snap is not constrained by TAM. It is constrained by audience mix, ad demand, and competition from platforms whose young-user reach is at least comparable and whose advertiser systems are currently growing faster. A valuation should not award a large regulatory option value: broad youth rules hit Snap directly, and the U.S. TikTok binary has receded.

The most defensible base case is **slight global advertising-share recovery**, driven by low-teens ad growth against a high-single-digit broad digital market. The bull case requires measurable budget transfer and North American stabilization. The bear case is not user collapse; it is continued growth in low-value users and subscriptions masking advertising-share erosion in the regions that matter most.

## Evidence limitations

- Platform user metrics use different identity, frequency and de-duplication rules.
- Official ARPU can include non-advertising revenue and different denominators.
- TikTok/ByteDance does not provide a public reconciled quarterly global financial/user bridge.
- Market forecasts differ materially in taxonomy and gross/net treatment.
- Snap regional revenue includes subscriptions; regional advertising share cannot be isolated.
- Survey “ever use,” daily use, time spent and platform-reported active users answer different questions.
- No paid, authenticated, app-account, or private advertiser data was used.
