# Snap valuation confidence-gap estimates

> **Purpose:** determine whether public filings, independent data, signed-in research services, publisher discovery, interviews, and transparent calculations can replace or bound the six missing inputs identified in the re-underwritten thesis.
>
> **Evidence rule:** reported facts, calculations, external estimates, and assumptions are kept separate. A proxy is not relabeled as a company disclosure. This memo updates the same-day draft; it is not personalized investment advice.

## Answer first

The additional work materially improves the valuation record. The biggest result is not an estimate: Snap's second-quarter 2026 Form 10-Q says average advertising price per impression **rose approximately 10% year over year**. The earlier draft incorrectly called second-quarter price undisclosed. With advertising revenue up 9.3%, delivered impressions were approximately flat to slightly down on a simple revenue/price identity. This is the first clear quarter of company-wide yield repair in the recent series.

Five other gaps can be narrowed, but not eliminated:

| Input | What public evidence now supports | What remains unknown | Evidence confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Subscription churn and economics | one company-selected retention uplift, plan-duration/category base rates, stock-flow acquisition sensitivities, and directionally accretive gross-margin evidence | absolute cohort retention, plan mix, geography, channel fees, partnership split, and subscription contribution margin | medium-low |
| Age-cohort retention | a steep cross-sectional adult age curve plus evidence of strong teen relationship use and recent 35+ growth | a longitudinal cohort curve, transition hazard, and age-by-revenue | low |
| Regional contribution | a Q2-reconciled ad-yield model showing base gross contribution around 72% / 60% / 13% and contribution after direct cash-like sales/support around 60% / 49% / 3% for North America / Europe / Rest of World | actual subscription geography and regional cloud, moderation, support, content, sales, and payment costs | medium for ordering/signs; medium-low for Rest-of-World point |
| Q2 advertising price | approximately +10% reported price and about -1% implied impressions; cost-per-install, cost-per-purchase, purchase volume, and incremental return remain separate outcome metrics | regional price/impression splits and whether the improvement persists after World Cup spending | high for the reported price; medium for the implication |
| Specs economics | $2,195 price, $200 refundable deposit, fall launch, more than $3bn cumulative spend, an activist estimate of about $500m annual cash spend, and transparent breakeven thresholds | bill of materials, preorder count, returns, production commitments, gross margin, and outside funding | medium-low |
| Legal settlement terms | Snap's $8m Breathitt County payment, no admission or product-change covenant, a separate insured $65m securities settlement, and disputed coverage on platform-harm claims | individual bellwether terms, a scalable school-district framework, coverage result, and remedies | medium for known cases; low for aggregate tail |

The overall valuation confidence moves from plain **medium** to **medium with materially better calibration**, but not to high. The probability-weighted value rises from **$7.48 to $7.74** because the reported advertising-price inflection shifts five probability points from bear to base. The bear, base, and bull values remain **$2.50, $7.75, and $14.25**; no forecast revenue or multiple was raised.

## 1. Subscription churn: a useful signal, not a Snap retention curve

### Reported facts

- Subscribers reached 24 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 71% year over year. Other Revenue was $232 million. Snap said the growing subscription business, built on existing engagement and infrastructure, should become increasingly accretive to overall gross margin. [Snap Q4 2025 investor letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000011/snapincq42025investorlet.htm)
- Direct-revenue subscribers passed 25 million and the business exceeded a $1 billion annualized run rate in February 2026. [Snap direct-revenue milestone](https://newsroom.snap.com/snap-1b-direct-revenue-25m-subscription)
- Other Revenue reached $316.5 million in the second quarter of 2026, up 85%. It includes subscriptions and partnerships; physical-product revenue was not material. Subscription revenue is reported gross and transaction-processing fees sit in cost of revenue. [Snap Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000052/snap-20260630.htm)
- Current plans include monthly and annual base subscriptions plus Family, Platinum, and Lens+ variants. Availability and price vary by region. [Snapchat plan documentation](https://help.snapchat.com/hc/en-us/articles/45845487406740-What-Snapchat-plans-are-available)
- Company-selected internal data shared with Reuters said subscribers using customization features retained at rates more than ten percentage points above other subscribers. Nearly 80% had set at least one custom chat wallpaper. The absolute rates, cohort windows, and mix-adjustment method were not disclosed, and usage selection can explain part of the association. [Reuters report distributed by MarketScreener](https://uk.marketscreener.com/news/snap-aims-to-boost-retention-with-new-features-for-snapchat-subscribers-ce7d5ddcdc8bff22)

### External base rate—not a Snap estimate

RevenueCat's 2026 subscription-app dataset reports median first-year retention of approximately 8% for monthly plans and 28% for annual plans; Social & Lifestyle annual retention was near 25%, and its first monthly renewal was approximately 42%. The dataset covers apps using RevenueCat, not Snap's subscriber ledger. Snap's social graph, Memories, pricing, regions, web checkout, and plan mix can produce a materially different result. [RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-utilities)

The defensible use is a stock-flow sensitivity. Starting with 24 million subscribers and ten million net additions during 2025:

| Illustrative annual base churn | Lost subscribers | Gross additions required for +10m net |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 20% | 4.8m | 14.8m |
| 40% | 9.6m | 19.6m |
| 60% | 14.4m | 24.4m |

These are identities, not estimates of Snap's churn. They show why rapid net growth can coexist with high consumer-subscription churn and why subscriber stock alone cannot justify a software-as-a-service multiple.

### Valuation sensitivity

The base sum-of-the-parts model has $1.435 billion of next-four-quarter Other Revenue and a 3.0-times multiple. At 1.92 billion diluted shares:

- each **1.0-times** change in the Other Revenue multiple changes value by about **$0.75 per share**;
- each **$100 million** change in Other Revenue at 3.0 times changes value by about **$0.16 per share**.

The new evidence supports keeping a premium to distressed advertising. It does not support moving to an enterprise-software multiple because the most valuation-sensitive subscription fields remain unreported.

## 2. Age-out behavior: the curve is steep, but it is cross-sectional

Pew's 2025 United States adult survey reports Snapchat use of 58% among ages 18–29, 31% among 30–49, 13% among 50–64, and 4% among 65+. Relative to the 18–29 rate, the older groups are approximately 53%, 22%, and 7%. [Pew Social Media Fact Sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/)

That is a useful directional age curve. It is **not** a transition probability: the age bands are different birth cohorts observed at one time. Product history, adoption timing, network composition, survey error, and true age-out all contribute. Pew's teen work and Ofcom's passive measurement also show unusually relationship-heavy and high-frequency youth use, while Snap says the United States audience broadened sequentially in the second quarter, led by people aged 35 and older. [Pew teen platform study](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/04/15/teens-experiences-on-tiktok-instagram-and-snapchat/) [Ofcom Online Nation 2025](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/research-and-data/online-research/online-nation/2025/online-nations-report-2025.pdf?v=409414) [Snap Q2 2026 prepared remarks](https://s25.q4cdn.com/442043304/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/Q2-2026-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)

The valuation should therefore retain the current high-value-user caution. The evidence rejects both extreme claims—"everyone ages out" and "the teen graph is permanent"—without identifying an annual retention rate.

## 3. Regional contribution: country ad prices narrow the cost allocation

The uniform-cost stress test originally placed gross contribution near 75% in North America, 60% in Europe, and breakeven in Rest of World. It was useful but deliberately punitive to Rest of World because it allocated every infrastructure dollar equally per daily user.

A dedicated [regional advertising economics memo](2026-08-21-regional-ad-economics.md) now adds:

- 2026 country- and operating-system-level Snap acquisition eCPMs;
- exact Snap campaign CPMs and format effects;
- a current Meta country-CPM panel and Reddit objective/geography cross-checks;
- country-level Snapchat+ App Store prices;
- an exact bridge to Q2 reported Cost of Revenue, gross profit, Sales and Marketing, and operating loss; and
- a 2,673-case sensitivity grid.

The improved base model allocates Q2 infrastructure 60% by daily-active-user share and 40% by inferred monetized-impression share, using broad Europe/North-America and Rest-of-World/North-America advertiser-yield indices. It allocates Other Revenue 45% / 25% / 30% and 75% of cash-like Sales and Marketing to regions. The results are estimates—not reported regional profit:

| Region | Base gross contribution margin | Middle 80% of grid | Base after direct cash-like sales/support | Middle 80% of grid |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| North America | 72.1% | 69.7%–74.3% | 60.0% | 57.2%–63.2% |
| Europe | 59.9% | 56.1%–62.6% | 48.8% | 44.6%–52.1% |
| Rest of World | 13.0% | 4.2%–24.2% | 3.2% | -6.2%–14.0% |

The grid is mechanical, not probabilistic. If all GAAP Sales and Marketing is allocated to regions by estimated advertising revenue, the base margins are approximately 52% / 42% / -3%.

This supports a stronger but still bounded conclusion: North America and Europe are very likely positive at gross and direct-contribution levels; Rest of World is probably positive before sales/support and approximately breakeven after it. Confidence in the ordering and signs rises to **medium**, while the Rest-of-World point remains medium-low because subscription geography, infrastructure workload, moderation, support, and publisher costs remain undisclosed.

## 4. Q2 advertising yield: the missing figure was in the 10-Q

Snap's filing reports:

- Q2 2026 advertising revenue of $1,282.5 million versus $1,173.5 million, up 9.29%;
- an approximately **10% year-over-year increase in average cost per advertising impression** for the quarter;
- first-half advertising impressions up approximately 8% and first-half average price down approximately 2%.

Source: [Snap Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000052/snap-20260630.htm).

Snap's "average cost per advertising impression" is the company-wide publisher-side yield underlying effective cost per thousand impressions (**eCPM**). It is not an advertiser's campaign-level quoted cost per thousand impressions (**CPM**), cost per acquisition (**CPA**), or return on advertising spend (**ROAS**).

Using the standard identity:

```text
Impression growth ≈ (1 + advertising-revenue growth) / (1 + price growth) - 1

Q2 ≈ 1.0929 / 1.10 - 1 = -0.65%
```

Because Snap rounded price to "approximately 10%" and mix sits inside the average-price measure, the correct description is **roughly flat to slightly negative impressions**, not a precise -0.65% observation.

This is materially more constructive than the earlier draft. Q2 reversed the recent pattern of double-digit impression growth and falling price. It also introduces a new caution: 9% advertising growth came from price/mix while volume did not grow. Sustainable low-to-mid-teens advertising growth now requires price repair to persist, impression delivery to resume without dilution, or both.

Cost per install (-8%), cost per purchase (-18%), app-purchase volume (+128%), and the issuer-sponsored incremental-return study are advertiser-outcome metrics. They support a mechanism for higher demand but are not substitutes for the company-wide price disclosure. [Snap Q2 2026 prepared remarks](https://s25.q4cdn.com/442043304/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/Q2-2026-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)

## 5. Specs: spending can be bounded better than unit economics

### Reported and attributed facts

- Snap says it spent more than $3 billion over 11 years on augmented reality. [Snap 2025 Specs announcement](https://newsroom.snap.com/launch-specs-2026?lang=en-GB)
- Specs are priced at $2,195 with a $200 refundable deposit and expected to ship in the fall in the United States, United Kingdom, and France. [Snap Specs announcement](https://investor.snap.com/news/news-details/2026/Snap-Inc--Debuts-SPECS-Augmented-Reality-Glasses-to-Make-Computing-More-Human/default.aspx)
- Irenic Capital, an activist investor, estimates cumulative spending above $3.5 billion and current annual cash spending around $500 million. This is an interested-party estimate, not a Snap segment disclosure. [Irenic presentation announcement](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260331059373/en/Irenic-Sends-Letter-to-Snap-Inc.-Co-Founder-and-CEO-Evan-Spiegel-and-Issues-Presentation-Outlining-Actionable-Steps-to-Unlock-Value)
- Snap's $2.6 billion non-cancelable commitments cover hosting, leases, content/developer partners, and other items. The filing does not attribute an amount to Specs. Physical-product revenue was not material in Q2. [Snap Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000052/snap-20260630.htm)
- In a checked CNBC interview, Evan Spiegel described the likely initial buyers as early adopters and developers and cited more than 450,000 developers using Snap augmented-reality tools. That developer count is not a preorder count. [CNBC interview, 2:13–2:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h9OzwbeQ_6g)
- Financial Times search metadata described investor pressure around costs and the $2,195 launch. No subscriber-article body was ingested. [Financial Times search result](https://www.ft.com/content/f0912c3f-a11d-4517-b61a-5d2f649d89bb)

### Breakeven thresholds—not a forecast

At a $2,195 selling price, annual unit volume needed to cover illustrative annual fixed cash spending is:

| Illustrative annual fixed cash spend | 20% hardware gross margin | 40% hardware gross margin |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $250m | ~569,000 units | ~285,000 units |
| $500m | ~1.14m units | ~569,000 units |

The $500 million row is anchored to Irenic's estimate; the $250 million row is a sensitivity, not a sourced spending forecast. Neither includes returns, working capital, channel costs, future research, or a service-revenue stream. The table demonstrates the hurdle: a premium early-adopter product does not self-fund a large research program at tens of thousands of units.

The current valuation remains appropriately conservative: no hardware profit is forecast, and Specs carries only $0/$250m/$750m of net option value across bear/base/bull cases.

## 6. Legal terms: one important settlement is public

Reuters obtained the Breathitt County settlement agreements through a public-records request. Snap paid **$8 million**; TikTok paid $8 million, Meta $9 million, and YouTube $2.01 million. The companies admitted no liability and agreed to no platform changes. The settlement covers one roughly 1,600-student rural district; plaintiffs said approximately 1,200 other school-district cases remained. [Reuters report distributed by Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/meta-paid-9-million-to-settle-kentucky-school-districts-lawsuit-over-social-media-harms-records-show-4717510) [Bloomberg Law report](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/business-and-practice/social-media-giants-to-pay-27-million-to-settle-school-lawsuit)

At 1.92 billion diluted shares, Snap's $8 million payment is approximately **$0.004 per share**. It is far below the existing probability-weighted United States legal reserve and supports the view that a small bellwether can settle for a manageable amount. It cannot be multiplied by 1,200: district size, alleged costs, evidence, plaintiffs, and remedies vary radically, and the next scheduled district is much larger.

Separately, Snap's $65 million securities settlement over Apple tracking changes was covered by insurance and received final approval in April 2026. That is not evidence that platform-harm claims are insured; Snap's filing says carriers dispute defense or indemnity for some or all of those claims. Individual youth-harm bellwether settlements remain confidential. [Snap Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000052/snap-20260630.htm)

The existing central regulatory/legal reserve remains appropriate. The new $8 million datapoint narrows one node of the distribution but does not resolve aggregate cash exposure or product-remedy risk.

## 7. Valuation update and confidence

The Q2 advertising-price disclosure is the only new evidence strong enough to change scenario probabilities. It validates the base case's required direction, but one quarter is insufficient for the bull case because impressions were roughly flat and World Cup spending helped the quarter.

| Scenario | Previous probability | Updated probability | Value per share |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Bear | 30% | **25%** | $2.50 |
| Base | 50% | **55%** | $7.75 |
| Bull | 20% | **20%** | $14.25 |
| **Probability-weighted** | **$7.48** | **$7.74** | — |

The updated six-month probability-weighted checkpoint is **$6.79**. The twelve-month weighted value is 48.5% above the $5.21 reference price.

Confidence remains below high because:

- +10% advertising price has been observed for one quarter, while volume was approximately flat;
- subscriber retention evidence is selected and relative, not an absolute cohort curve;
- the regional result is a hybrid ad-yield/cost-allocation model, not a company disclosure, and the Rest-of-World point remains wide;
- the Specs annual-spend figure is an activist estimate and unit economics remain absent;
- one $8 million school settlement does not price the wider litigation or remedy tail;
- founder control, dilution, high-value-user decline, debt, and regulatory product changes still widen the distribution.

The correct wording is therefore **medium confidence, materially improved from the earlier draft**. The new work increases confidence in the central scenario calibration more than it increases the precision of the point target.

## Research-process note

This pass used public web search, Snap and Securities and Exchange Commission pages, the user's signed-in Chrome session, Computer Use for visible application state, Financial Times search metadata, a checked YouTube transcript/playback passage, and two narrowly scoped Gemini Deep Research runs. Paid Financial Times article text was not ingested. No Bloomberg, Reuters, or Wall Street Journal source was treated as evidence without an accessible public report or corroborating primary record.

The first Gemini report was useful for issue discovery and independently converged on a regional proxy. It also incorrectly attributed 19% impression growth to Snap and stated a $34.5 million Kentucky settlement. The second run converged on a hybrid user/ad-delivery cost allocation but exposed unsupported price bands and regional “net” margins; its final-report panel did not render cleanly. Primary filings, underlying publisher pages, and the deterministic local model replaced those claims. Local-only captures remain under `.local/captures/gemini/`; Gemini output itself is not evidence.

No new repository skill was created. The existing company-research, Gemini, Financial Times discovery, YouTube, Chrome, and Computer Use workflows covered the task; the missing step was source reconciliation, not a repeatable tooling gap.
