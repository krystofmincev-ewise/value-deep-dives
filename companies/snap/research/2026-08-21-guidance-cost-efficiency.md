# Snap guidance behavior and cost-efficiency revalidation

**Evidence cutoff:** 2026-08-21
**Purpose:** test whether Snap habitually guides conservatively and whether serving almost 500 million daily users is becoming economically more efficient.
**Status:** analyst revalidation; not a stand-alone valuation or investment instruction.

> **Plain-English reading rule:** Financial terms are written out before their acronym. A **fact** comes from Snap or a filing, a **calculation** is arithmetic from those facts, and an **assumption** is an input to the forward model.

## The short answer

Two statements can both be true:

1. **Snap has recently guided adjusted operating profit conservatively.** In the four guided quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026, actual adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (adjusted EBITDA) exceeded the top of the prior range every time, by approximately 15% to 35%.
2. **Revenue guidance has been much tighter.** Revenue was usually at or only slightly above the top of the range. The second quarter of 2026 was the one clearly material revenue beat.

That distinction matters. A sensible third-quarter model should allow for better cost performance than the $300 million to $350 million adjusted EBITDA guide, but it should not mechanically assume another large revenue surprise.

Snap's cost structure is also improving in a way that the earlier thesis did not emphasize enough. Revenue grew 19% year over year in the second quarter of 2026 while the total adjusted cost structure grew 4%. Adjusted gross margin reached 58%, up seven percentage points, even while Snap increased artificial-intelligence and machine-learning infrastructure investment. The strongest evidence is therefore not simply “500 million users are cheap to serve.” It is that **cost per daily user has stayed broadly stable while revenue per daily user and gross margin improved**.

### What the table does—and does not—show

- **Fact:** Adjusted EBITDA beat the top of guidance in all four quarters. The median beat was approximately 24%.
- **Fact:** Revenue was within approximately 0.4% of the high end in three quarters. The second quarter of 2026 beat by approximately 3.2%.
- **Inference:** Management has recently left more cushion in costs and adjusted profit than in revenue.
- **Limitation:** Four observations are not a timeless management law. The period also contains major cost actions, improving gross margin, changing direct-revenue mix, geopolitical disruption, and a restructuring.
- **Expectation problem:** Once investors recognize a pattern, the market may compare the result with expectations above formal guidance. Beating the official range can still disappoint the share price.

### Implication for the third quarter of 2026

Snap guides third-quarter revenue to $1.70 billion to $1.74 billion and adjusted EBITDA to $300 million to $350 million. Personnel savings should be more fully visible from this quarter.

The re-underwrite should therefore use:

- **Bear:** revenue below or near the low end and adjusted EBITDA around the low end;
- **Base:** revenue modestly above the midpoint or high end, with adjusted EBITDA moderately above $350 million;
- **Bull:** a clear revenue beat led by advertising demand—not only direct revenue—with adjusted EBITDA above the range and no new infrastructure-cost increase.

It would be too aggressive to apply the historical median 24% profit beat mechanically. That would imply roughly $435 million of adjusted EBITDA, even though the new guide already incorporates much of the known restructuring benefit.

## 2. What changed in the cost base?

### The restructuring claim, reconciled

Snap stated that its April 2026 restructuring would reduce the **annualized cost structure by more than $500 million in the second half of 2026**. This is a run-rate statement, not a promise that 2026 cash flow rises by $500 million.

The observable changes include:

- approximately 1,000 affected personnel, including a planned reduction of roughly 16% of full-time employees;
- more than 300 open roles closed;
- official full-time employee count declining from 5,381 at the end of the first quarter to 4,723 at the end of the second quarter, a 12.2% sequential decline;
- the 2026 adjusted operating-expense outlook falling from approximately $3.0 billion in February to approximately $2.75 billion after the restructuring;
- the 2026 stock-based-compensation outlook falling from approximately $1.2 billion to approximately $1.05 billion.

The last two items explain approximately $400 million of the change in disclosed annual expense guides. The larger “more than $500 million” run-rate can include payroll taxes, benefits, contractor or other personnel costs, and timing differences. Snap does not publish a complete line-by-line bridge, so the remaining amount should not be invented.

### Why $500 million of savings is not $500 million of free cash flow

Savings can be absorbed by:

- artificial-intelligence and machine-learning computing;
- legal and safety compliance;
- Specs development and launch costs already included in the operating outlook;
- higher cash interest on the 6.875% senior notes;
- transaction timing, severance, and working capital;
- taxes as the company approaches profitability.

Stock-based compensation is also partly non-cash in the current period. A reduction in stock-based compensation improves owner economics, but it does not create a dollar-for-dollar near-term increase in operating cash flow.

The important observation is stability, not a dramatic decline. Snap is serving more users and spending more on model training, but infrastructure cost per daily user has remained around the low-to-mid $0.80s per quarter.

### Annualized second-quarter unit picture

Using the midpoint of the 2026 infrastructure guide and the second-quarter user snapshot:

> `Annual infrastructure cost per daily user ≈ $1.675 billion / 493 million = $3.40`

Second-quarter average revenue per daily user was $3.25 for the quarter, or approximately $13.00 annualized if the quarter were representative.

> `Infrastructure share of annualized revenue per daily user ≈ $3.40 / $13.00 = 26%`

All other adjusted cost of revenue is guided to 16%–17% of revenue. At the 16.5% midpoint, the simplified global-average unit bridge is:

| Annualized amount per daily user | Approximate value |
| --- | ---: |
| Revenue | $13.00 |
| Infrastructure | ($3.40) |
| Other adjusted cost of revenue | ($2.15) |
| **Adjusted gross profit before operating expenses** | **$7.45** |

This is not a regional profit table. Snap does not disclose infrastructure, content, safety, support, or sales cost by geography. The global average cannot prove whether a Rest-of-World user with $1.00 of quarterly revenue is profitable. Marginal infrastructure cost can be lower than average fixed cost, and engagement intensity differs by region.

### Gross margin and operating leverage

In the second quarter of 2026:

- revenue increased by $254 million year over year;
- adjusted EBITDA increased by $208 million;
- the simple incremental adjusted-EBITDA flow-through was approximately 82%;
- adjusted gross margin rose from approximately 51% to 58%;
- the total adjusted cost structure increased only 4% while revenue increased 19%.

> `Incremental adjusted-EBITDA flow-through = increase in adjusted EBITDA / increase in revenue`
> `= $208 million / $254 million ≈ 82%`

This is strong operating evidence. It is not the same as owner earnings because adjusted EBITDA excludes stock-based compensation, depreciation and amortization, interest, restructuring, and other items. Snap still reported a $164 million loss under Generally Accepted Accounting Principles in the quarter.

### Revenue per full-time employee

A rough annualized comparison is:

| Quarter-end snapshot | Annualized revenue / full-time employees |
| --- | ---: |
| First quarter of 2026 | approximately $1.14 million |
| Second quarter of 2026 | approximately $1.35 million |

This approximately 19% increase is directionally encouraging but mixes seasonality with a restructuring that occurred during the quarter. It should be monitored over four quarters rather than treated as a completed productivity gain.

## 4. What should change in valuation?

The cost evidence argues against a model that assumes Snap remains permanently stuck near its old margin structure. It supports:

- moderately higher third-quarter and next-four-quarter adjusted EBITDA in the base case;
- a credible path to an adjusted gross margin around 60%;
- a lower probability of near-term liquidity stress;
- a higher multiple if revenue growth remains double digit and free cash flow per diluted share grows.

It does **not** justify:

- adding the full $500 million to free cash flow;
- ignoring approximately $1.05 billion of annual stock-based compensation;
- assuming infrastructure cost falls while management is deliberately increasing investment;
- treating a formal guidance beat as a surprise without checking market expectations;
- assuming every international user is profitable or unprofitable without regional cost data.

## 5. Scorecard for the next two quarters

| Question | Constructive evidence | Warning evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is guidance still conservative? | Revenue and adjusted EBITDA exceed the ranges for operating reasons | Profit only meets the guide despite the savings ramp |
| Is the user base getting cheaper to serve? | Infrastructure cost per daily user is stable or lower while model performance improves | Infrastructure guide rises again without matching revenue or gross-margin gains |
| Are savings reaching shareholders? | Free cash flow per diluted share rises and repurchases are funded from free cash flow | Cash falls or net debt rises while repurchases merely absorb awards |
| Is artificial intelligence improving real output? | Reliability, advertiser results, and revenue per employee improve | Commit counts rise while outages, ad yield, or product quality deteriorate |
| Is the margin gain durable? | Adjusted gross margin reaches approximately 60% without starving growth | Gross margin falls below 55% or legal/safety costs rebuild operating expense |

## Bottom line

The earlier integrated model was probably too cautious about the **near-term adjusted-profit beat**, but not obviously too cautious about **revenue**. Snap's current efficiency record deserves meaningful credit: roughly stable infrastructure cost per daily user, sharply better gross margin, high incremental flow-through, and a smaller operating organization. The valuation should be revised upward only to the extent these gains produce durable free cash flow **per diluted share**, rather than being recycled into stock dilution, litigation, or an undisclosed Specs cost center.
