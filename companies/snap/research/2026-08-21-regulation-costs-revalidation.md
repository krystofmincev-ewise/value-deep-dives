# Snap regulation and cost-structure revalidation

> **Purpose:** re-underwrite the child-safety regulation, litigation, and cost-efficiency risks that matter to Snap's next 12 months and longer-term value. This is analyst research, not legal advice or a trade instruction.
>
> **Reader rule:** a **Fact** is reported by Snap, a court, a legislature, or a regulator; a **Calculation** is arithmetic from stated inputs; an **Assumption** is an uncertain model input. Full terms appear before acronyms at first use in this memo.
>
> **Number key:** `$m` means millions of United States dollars; `$bn` means billions of United States dollars; `A$` means Australian dollars. Percentages in scenario tables are analyst probabilities, not company guidance.

## The one-minute answer

The risk is material, but the evidence does **not** support treating a global Snapchat ban, a European fine, or a U.S. mass settlement as certain.

- **Australia is real but small at company scale.** The under-16 account restriction took effect on December 10, 2025. Snap unsuccessfully sought a messaging-service exclusion, publicly testified against its designation, locked more than 450,000 accounts, and continues to criticize the regime. Snap has not disclosed an Australian user, revenue, or profit loss. A bottom-up estimate suggests approximately **$4 million to $9 million of annual revenue directly removed**, before wider cohort effects. Most of that loss is already in the second-quarter (Q2) 2026 run-rate and must not be deducted from the valuation again.
- **The United States is the largest uncertain liability.** Snap has confidentially settled two individual bellwether cases. Public-record reporting shows Snap paid $8 million to settle the first school-district case set for trial, without admitting liability or agreeing to platform changes. Snap's second-quarter filing listed two individual bellwethers for October 2026, but an August 20 report says P.M.-Y. requested dismissal of her remaining claims against Snap, Meta Platforms, and Google. The exact number of October cases still involving Snap is therefore unconfirmed; state attorney-general trials in Nevada and New Mexico remain scheduled for August 2027. One manageable school-district amount does not price the remaining cases. Snap's insurance carriers dispute at least some coverage, increasing the cash-tail risk. State product-design laws are becoming a more immediate operating cost than federal law.
- **The European Union proceeding is serious and early.** The European Commission opened a formal Digital Services Act (DSA) investigation in March 2026 covering age assurance, grooming and criminal recruitment, unsafe defaults, illegal or age-restricted goods, and reporting/redress design. The statutory 6% worldwide-turnover ceiling is a maximum, not a forecast. Commitments and product changes are more likely in the next 12 months than a maximum fine.
- **The United Kingdom is the clearest new forward user risk.** The government intends an under-16 social-media restriction from spring 2027 and has explicitly named Snapchat. Regulations and parliamentary approval are still required. Snap's unusually high youth engagement makes its percentage exposure plausibly larger than Meta's diversified portfolio, although the measure is sector-wide.
- **Cost efficiency has genuinely improved, but not because each user has become dramatically cheaper to compute.** Q2 2026 revenue grew 19% year over year while Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) cost of revenue grew only about 2%, lifting GAAP gross margin from 51.4% to 58.2%. Infrastructure cost per daily active user (DAU) has been broadly stable near $0.83 to $0.85 per quarter since 2024. Snap is getting more revenue and gross profit from a roughly stable compute load per user while serving 493 million DAU.
- **The April 2026 savings claim needs exact language.** The action affected about 1,000 people, including a planned 16% reduction in full-time employees (FTE), and closed more than 300 open roles. Management targets **more than $500 million of annualized cost-base savings by the second half (H2)**. That is neither a 20% FTE cut nor $500 million of immediate 2026 cash flow.

### Central probability-weighted valuation reserve

**Assumption:** use a 1.6 times enterprise-value-to-revenue multiple and 1.90 billion diluted shares only to translate risk into valuation language. This is a risk module, not a replacement for the full valuation.

> `Value impact = incremental cash cost or reserve + recurring annual revenue loss × 1.6`
>
> `Per-share impact = value impact / 1.90 billion diluted shares`

Cash costs are treated dollar for dollar. The bridge gives no credit for tax deductions or insurance recoveries, making it conservative on those two dimensions.

After removing effects already in the current run-rate and applying overlap haircuts, the central probability-weighted regulatory/legal reserve is approximately **$180 million to $260 million**, or roughly **$0.09 to $0.14 per diluted share**. The 12-month adverse tail is approximately **$600 million to $1.2 billion**, or **$0.32 to $0.63 per share**, but that tail is not the expected outcome. It is driven mainly by U.S. settlements/remedies and a European Union enforcement surprise.

This reserve should be reduced if the main Snap model already includes elevated legal, safety, age-assurance, and moderation expense. Otherwise the same cost is counted twice.

## 1. Australia: a real restriction, not an existential ban

### What the law actually does

**Fact:** under the Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act 2024, age-restricted platforms must take **reasonable steps** to prevent Australian residents under 16 from creating or keeping accounts from December 10, 2025. It is not strict liability, and children or parents are not fined. The eSafety Commissioner identifies Snapchat, Facebook, Instagram, Threads, TikTok, Twitch, X, YouTube, Kick, and Reddit as covered platforms. [eSafety overview](https://www.esafety.gov.au/about-us/industry-regulation/social-media-age-restrictions) [Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2024A00127/asmade/2024-12-10/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html)

**Fact:** the rules were amended in March 2026 to catch services with recommender and/or logged-in features. Standalone messaging services can be excluded, but messaging attached to social-media features can remain covered. [Current legislative rules](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2025L00889/2026-03-26/2026-03-26/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html)

**Fact:** the current corporate maximum is 150,000 penalty units, which eSafety states is **A$54.6 million**. A court, not a headline, determines any penalty. A later proposal to strengthen enforcement and increase penalties was still before the Senate at the cutoff; it is not current law. [eSafety civil-penalty explanation](https://www.esafety.gov.au/about-us/industry-regulation/social-media-age-restrictions) [Senate inquiry](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Committees/Senate/Environment_and_Communications/StrengtheningSMMA48P)

### Did Snap fight it?

Yes—publicly and through the legislative process.

- **Fact:** Snap argued that Snapchat's primary purpose is private messaging among existing friends and sought an exclusion similar to standalone messaging services. Its submission and October 2025 parliamentary testimony challenged the fit between Snapchat and the law's policy theory. [Australian government freedom-of-information release containing Snap material](https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/foi26-007-documents-released.pdf) [Snap parliamentary testimony](https://values.snap.com/news/au-u16-parliamentary-testimony)
- **Fact:** after the government rejected that interpretation, Snap implemented account locking and age assurance. The methods described to users include identity documents, on-device facial-age estimation, bank-based ConnectID, and AgeKeys. [Snap Australia account-restriction support](https://help.snapchat.com/hc/en-gb/articles/43354851383828-Account-Restrictions-for-Australia-Users)
- **Fact:** Snap says it locked more than 415,000 suspected under-16 accounts by the end of January 2026 and later described the total as more than 450,000. It continues to argue that the policy displaces minors to less regulated services and has not achieved its intended result. That argument is evidence of opposition, not independent proof that the law fails. [Snap policy response](https://newsroom.snap.com/australia-social-media-ban-high-stakes-experiment?lang=fil-PH) [Snap-commissioned follow-up](https://values.snap.com/news/new-research-au-u16-ban-wont-make-teens-safer)

The corrected conclusion is therefore: **Snap lost the policy argument, complied, and continues to push back.** The record does not support saying it was passive.

### Is compliance working?

The answer is mixed and still under investigation.

**Fact:** eSafety reported that covered platforms collectively removed or restricted 4.7 million accounts by mid-December 2025, followed by more than 300,000 additional accounts. This is a cross-platform account total, not a count of unique children or DAU. [eSafety March compliance update](https://www.esafety.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-03/SocialMediaMinimumAgeComplianceUpdateMarch2026.pdf)

**Fact:** in eSafety's early parent research, 69.4% of parents whose 10–15-year-old had used Snapchat before the start date still believed the child retained an account. eSafety said it was investigating Snapchat, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube. This is survey evidence about reported access, not a final legal finding that Snap failed the reasonable-steps test. [eSafety March compliance update](https://www.esafety.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-03/SocialMediaMinimumAgeComplianceUpdateMarch2026.pdf)

**Fact:** in the three-month evaluation, 204 surveyed 10–15-year-olds who still held Snapchat accounts gave several explanations: no age check, an account registered as 16+, an inaccurate age-assurance result, workaround behavior, a new account after deactivation, parental help, or a virtual private network. These reasons may overlap and should not be summed into one failure rate. [eSafety July early evaluation](https://www.esafety.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-07/Early-days-early-insights-SMMA-evaluation-three-months-July-2026.pdf)

**Interpretation:** Snap has a defensible reasonable-steps case because it removed a large number of accounts and deployed several verification paths. The high reported persistence rate gives eSafety a credible basis to demand stronger detection, repeat-account controls, and audit evidence. Both can be true at once.

### What loss is observable?

**Fact:** Snap has not disclosed Australian DAU, monthly active users, average revenue per user, revenue, subscription revenue, advertiser loss, or compliance cost. Australia is inside the Rest of World reporting region. The Q2 2026 Form 10-Q mentions the law as a risk but records no Australia-specific charge or material revenue impact. [Snap Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000052/snap-20260630.htm)

**Fact:** Snap's advertising tools indicated an Australian advertising reach of approximately 8.27 million people in early 2025, equal to about 30.8% of the population. Advertising reach is not DAU or monthly active users and can change with tool methodology. [DataReportal Australia 2025](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-australia)

**Fact:** the Australian online advertising market reached A$18.4 billion in 2025, with online video at A$5.4 billion. The total market provides a reasonableness check, not Snap's market share. [Interactive Advertising Bureau Australia](https://www.iabaustralia.com.au/news/internet-advertising-spend-increases-11-5-to-reach-18-4-billion-in-2025-video-now-represents-29-of-total-expenditure/)

### Bottom-up revenue estimate

**Assumption:** annual Snap revenue per Australian ad-reachable account is $12 to $20. This brackets global annualized Q2 2026 average revenue per user while allowing Australia to monetize above the Rest of World average but recognizing that ad reach is closer to a reachable-audience measure than a DAU measure.

> `Estimated Australia annual revenue = 8.27m ad-reachable accounts × $12–$20`
>
> `Estimated Australia annual revenue = approximately $99m–$165m`

**Assumption:** a locked under-16 account was worth $8 to $18 annually before the law. Minors generally have less purchasing power, more advertising restrictions, and potentially lower conversion value, but Australia is a high-income ad market.

> `Direct annual revenue removed = 450,000 locked accounts × $8–$18`
>
> `Direct annual revenue removed = approximately $3.6m–$8.1m`

Adding subscription loss and some adult age-assurance abandonment gives a reasonable **gross annual revenue-loss range of $4 million to $9 million**. This is approximately 2% to 9% of the inferred Australian revenue range and about 0.1% of Snap's global trailing revenue.

This estimate is deliberately wider than an account-count multiplication might suggest because:

- the 450,000 figure is accounts, not unique people, DAU, or ad-exposed users;
- under-16 monetization is not disclosed;
- some locked accounts may have been dormant, duplicated, or quickly restored after proving age;
- adult verification friction can lose higher-value users;
- losing a school-age cohort may weaken the friend graph and future adult monetization.

### Australia scenario model

| Scenario | Probability | Revenue effect | Incremental cash cost/fine | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Compliant / contained | 50% | $4m–$7m gross annual loss, already mostly in run-rate | $2m–$5m compliance | no material public sanction; iterative controls |
| Stronger remediation | 35% | $7m–$15m gross annual loss; $1m–$4m new from current run-rate | $5m–$15m | more aggressive rechecks, repeat-account controls, independent audit or undertaking |
| Adverse enforcement | 13% | $12m–$25m gross annual loss; $3m–$8m incremental | $15m–$35m | public proceeding and meaningful but sub-maximum penalty |
| Statutory-tail event | 2% | >$25m | near current A$54.6m maximum plus remediation | requires a serious proven failure; not the base case |

**Calculation:** the probability-weighted **incremental** 12-month revenue drag is approximately $2 million, with $5 million to $10 million of incremental compliance/enforcement cash exposure. At 1.6 times revenue, the incremental value effect is about **$8 million to $13 million**, roughly **$0.00 to $0.01 per share**.

**Longer-term risk:** if the ban prevents one full annual cohort from entering Snapchat and relationship graphs fail to re-form at 16, the cumulative value loss could exceed the immediate ad revenue. There is not enough retention or age-cohort data to quantify this responsibly. The United Kingdom scenario below partially captures that network risk; adding a separate global cohort penalty would double count it.

## 2. United States: litigation is now an operating variable

### Case posture at the cutoff

The most reliable current posture is Snap's Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, filed August 4.

**Fact:** since January 2022, Snap has faced claims that platform design is addictive and harmful, mostly concerning users under 18. Most cases sit in either the federal multidistrict litigation (MDL) in the Northern District of California or the California Judicial Council Coordinated Proceeding (JCCP) in Los Angeles. [Snap Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000052/snap-20260630.htm) [Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation transfer order](https://www.jpml.uscourts.gov/sites/jpml/files/MDL-3047-Transfer_Order-5-25%20%28Sullivan%29.pdf)

**Fact:** Snap confidentially settled its first and second JCCP individual bellwether cases. Snap's second-quarter filing listed two JCCP bellwethers involving Snap for October 2026. An August 20 report says P.M.-Y. requested dismissal of her remaining claims against Snap, Meta Platforms, and Google, potentially reducing Snap's count from the earlier filing; it also says two other teenagers remained in the wider October trial pool. A public primary docket order was not located by the cutoff, so the exact number still involving Snap is unconfirmed. In the completed first trial, Snap and TikTok had settled before trial; the jury awarded a total of $6 million against Meta and YouTube. The verdict is evidence that product-design claims can reach a jury, but it is not a Snap damages amount. [Snap Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000052/snap-20260630.htm) [Bloomberg Law P.M.-Y. report](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/health-law-and-business/app-addiction-plaintiff-drops-case-against-meta-google-snap) [Associated Press bellwether result](https://apnews.com/article/5e54075023d837ccdc76c4ca512e925d)

**Fact:** numerous school districts, municipalities, and tribal nations seek public-nuisance and related damages. Snap settled the first school-district case set for trial in June 2026; the next school-district trial involving Snap is scheduled for February 2027. Copies obtained under Kentucky public-records law show Snap paid Breathitt County School District **$8 million**, admitted no liability, and agreed to no platform changes. The settlement covers only one roughly 1,600-student district; plaintiffs said about 1,200 school-district cases remained. [Snap Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000052/snap-20260630.htm) [Reuters public-records report](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/meta-paid-9-million-to-settle-kentucky-school-districts-lawsuit-over-social-media-harms-records-show-4717510) [Bloomberg Law settlement report](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/business-and-practice/social-media-giants-to-pay-27-million-to-settle-school-lawsuit)

**Calculation:** $8 million is approximately $0.004 per 1.92 billion diluted shares. It supports the managed/contained portion of the distribution but cannot be multiplied by 1,200 because district size, alleged cost, evidence, and requested remedy vary dramatically.

**Fact:** state attorneys general allege combinations of addictive design, mature/harmful content, child-safety failures, sexual exploitation, misuse by bad actors, unlawful collection, privacy violations, and misleading safety marketing. Nevada and New Mexico trials involving Snap are scheduled for August 2027. [Snap Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000052/snap-20260630.htm) [Nevada complaint announcement](https://ag.nv.gov/News/PR/2024/Nevada_Attorney_General_Announces_Litigation_to_Address_Harms_Caused_by_Social_Media_Platforms_to_Nevada%E2%80%99s_Youth/) [New Mexico action](https://nmdoj.gov/press-release/attorney-general-raul-torrez-files-lawsuit-against-snap-inc-to-protect-children-from-sextortion-sexual-exploitation-and-other-harms/)

**Fact:** other risk clusters remain separate: fentanyl-death claims, in which several claims survived a 2024 pleading challenge; and federal/state cases alleging exploitation beginning on Roblox and continuing on services including Snapchat. These do not have the same causation or remedy theory as an addictive-feed case and should not be assigned one universal settlement rate. [Snap Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000052/snap-20260630.htm)

**Fact:** Snap sued insurance carriers in September 2025 for coverage of bodily-injury/property-damage claims. Several carriers deny a duty to defend or indemnify some or all claims. A motion to stay was set for August 6, 2026; the filing does not report the result. Insurance recovery is therefore uncertain and should not be assumed in the valuation. [Snap Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000052/snap-20260630.htm)

**Fact:** Snap says no loss range is reasonably estimable and that final disposition is not reasonably likely to have a material adverse effect. That is an accounting/legal assessment, not a guarantee. Repeated confidential settlements and disputed insurance justify an investor reserve despite the absence of a GAAP accrual.

**Fact:** on August 10, 2026, the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit rejected the platforms' attempt to appeal key rulings at this stage, allowing thousands of claims to continue. The court treated the appeal as premature; it did **not** decide that Snap is liable on the merits. This weakens a near-term procedural escape route without establishing damages. [Official Ninth Circuit opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2026/08/10/24-7265.pdf) [Reuters report on the decision](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/us-court-rules-meta-other-172206972.html)

### What remedies would actually matter?

Cash damages receive headlines, but product relief can damage revenue for longer. Plausible remedies include:

- stronger age assurance and recordkeeping;
- private-by-default youth accounts and tighter stranger-contact recommendations;
- chronological or followed-account feeds unless a parent consents to personalization;
- restrictions on Streaks, late-night notifications, autoplay, endless scroll, and other alleged engagement loops;
- clearer reporting and appeal tools for drugs, sexual exploitation, and harmful content;
- independent monitoring, audit, document-retention, and regulator reporting;
- restitution, civil penalties, settlement funds, or public-health programs.

Snap's economic sensitivity differs by surface. A restriction on Spotlight recommendation affects public-content engagement and ad inventory. A restriction on Streaks or friend notifications can affect the private-communication graph, where Snapchat is most differentiated. Stronger age assurance can also repel adults who do not want to provide identity or biometric evidence.

### Enacted state rules are nearer than federal law

**California.** Senate Bill 976, the Protecting Our Kids from Social Media Addiction Act, makes it unlawful to provide minors with addictive feeds and specified features without verifiable parental consent. The California Attorney General must complete age-assurance and consent rules by January 1, 2027. Litigation has narrowed/delayed parts of implementation, so the final operational scope remains a legal variable. [California Attorney General rulemaking page](https://oag.ca.gov/sb976) [Ninth Circuit opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2025/09/09/25-146.pdf)

**New York.** Final rules under the Stop Addictive Feeds Exploitation (SAFE) for Kids Act were released July 28, 2026 and take effect January 25, 2027. Under-18 users cannot receive algorithmically personalized feeds or midnight-to-6 a.m. notifications without parental consent. The law permits penalties up to $5,000 per violation. Platforms must offer an age-assurance alternative to government identification and meet accuracy, privacy, testing, and recordkeeping standards. [New York Attorney General final rules](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-and-governor-hochul-release-final-safe-kids-act-rules)

**Federal.** The House passed the Kids Internet and Digital Safety Act (KIDS Act), H.R. 7757, by 267–117 on June 29, 2026. Separately, the Senate Commerce Committee advanced an amended Kids Online Safety Act, S. 1748, by voice vote on August 5. The House and Senate texts are not the same, neither chamber has passed the other's version, and no federal act had been signed at the cutoff. [House Clerk roll call](https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/2026228) [Senate Commerce Committee action](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/press/rep/release/commerce-committee-advances-kids-online-safety-legislation/) [House-engrossed bill](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/BILLS-119hr7757eh/related)

**Probability judgment:** 75%–90% that Snap must implement material California/New York controls during the next 12 months, allowing for litigation or rule changes. There is a 30%–45% chance of some federal package becoming law before the end of this Congress, but a lower probability that it takes effect soon enough to materially affect the next-four-quarter financials.

### U.S. probability model

**Assumption:** Snap's United States revenue is approximately $3.1 billion to $3.4 billion within the North America segment. Snap does not disclose a standalone U.S. revenue line in its key performance table, so the range must not be treated as a reported figure.

| Scenario | Probability | Incremental 12-month cash/reserve | Annualized U.S. revenue drag | What has to happen |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Managed defense | 50% | $20m–$50m | 0%–0.3% | case-specific settlements; state implementation absorbed by existing safety work |
| Contained settlement cycle | 36% | $75m–$200m | 0.3%–1.0% | more bellwether settlements and moderate design/age-assurance changes |
| Adverse | 12% | $250m–$600m | 1.5%–3.0% | unfavorable discovery/trial pressure, weak insurance, multi-case framework or intrusive remedy |
| Severe tail | 2% | $750m–$1.5bn | 4%–7% | broad settlement framework or major injunction; not supported as the base case |

**Calculation:** using scenario midpoints produces approximately **$120 million to $150 million** of probability-weighted incremental cash/reserve and approximately **$20 million to $35 million** of annualized revenue drag.

> `Central U.S. value effect ≈ $120m–$150m + ($20m–$35m × 1.6)`
>
> `Central U.S. value effect ≈ $152m–$206m, or $0.08–$0.11 per diluted share`

The central estimate is an investor risk reserve, not a prediction that Snap will record that exact GAAP charge. Legal defense already embedded in general and administrative expense (G&A), and product work already embedded in research and development expense (R&D), should be excluded from incremental valuation deductions.

### Why the large tail is still low probability

- Snap settled bellwethers rather than taking early verdict risk. The two individual terms remain confidential; the first school-district amount is now public at $8 million for Snap.
- The $6 million first jury result against Meta and YouTube is meaningful precedent but modest relative to platform size and fact-specific.
- Causation, user-specific damages, statutory immunities, and product-versus-content distinctions remain contested.
- State cases and school-district claims have different remedies and cannot be mechanically multiplied by one settlement.
- Conversely, more than one settlement, remaining October trial pressure despite the exact Snap-specific count being unconfirmed, and disputed insurance make a zero-reserve position too optimistic.

## 3. European Union: formal investigation, maximum fine is not the forecast

### What is being investigated

**Fact:** on March 26, 2026, the European Commission opened formal proceedings into Snapchat's compliance with child-protection obligations under the Digital Services Act. The proceeding followed Snap's 2023–2025 risk assessments and an October 2025 request for information, and incorporates work with Dutch and German authorities. [European Commission announcement](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-investigates-snapchats-compliance-child-protection-rules-under-digital-services-act) [European Commission press release](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_723/IP_26_723_EN.pdf)

The five disclosed areas are:

1. whether self-declared age and other age-assurance measures are effective;
2. grooming and recruitment of minors for criminal purposes;
3. default settings and design, including friend recommendations and push notifications;
4. access to illegal or age-restricted goods such as drugs, vapes, and alcohol;
5. illegal-content reporting, possible manipulative design, complaint handling, and redress.

**Fact:** Snap's Q2 filing describes the proceeding as early stage, says it is cooperating, and states that the outcome and loss range cannot be predicted. Possible outcomes include fines and orders to change or stop product features or practices in the European Union. [Snap Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000052/snap-20260630.htm)

### Fine ceiling versus likely remedy

**Fact:** the Digital Services Act allows a fine of up to 6% of annual worldwide turnover for an infringement. Applied mechanically to Snap's 2025 revenue of $5.93 billion, 6% is approximately $356 million. Currency translation, the relevant turnover base, gravity, duration, cooperation, and the actual finding would determine any real amount. [Digital Services Act, Article 74](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A32022R2065) [Snap 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000013/snap-20251231.htm)

> `Illustrative statutory ceiling = $5.93bn × 6% = approximately $356m`

That calculation is a **ceiling marker**, not an expected-value input. At an early stage, commitments, audit requirements, and product modifications are more plausible than a maximum fine. Interim measures are possible if the Commission considers urgent harm present, but none had been announced at the cutoff.

### Do not count the youth-targeted-ad restriction twice

**Fact:** the Digital Services Act already prohibits profiling-based advertising to minors when a platform knows with reasonable certainty that the user is a minor. Snap said that from August 14, 2023 most targeting and optimization options were no longer available for advertisers reaching under-18 users in the European Union and United Kingdom. Current support material says 13–17-year-olds receive contextual ads using essential information rather than personalization. [Snap 2023 advertising update](https://values.snap.com/news/updating-our-advertising-in-europe-and-uk-2023) [Snap Digital Services Act frequently asked questions](https://help.snapchat.com/hc/en-gb/articles/18416565314964-Frequently-Asked-Questions-About-the-European-Digital-Services-Act)

**Conclusion:** the large first-order targeting loss should already be in Europe's historical average revenue per user and current advertising yield. The new forward risk is better age assurance, safer defaults/contact recommendations, content-sale controls, reporting/redress, and any sanction—not a second full removal of targeted minor ads.

### European Union exposure and scenarios

**Fact:** Snap reported $353.8 million of Q2 2026 Europe revenue and 98 million European DAU, but its Europe region includes the United Kingdom and other non-European-Union countries. It does not disclose European Union revenue.

**Assumption:** 65%–75% of trailing Europe revenue is European Union revenue, implying roughly $850 million to $1.0 billion annually.

| Scenario | Probability | Incremental cash cost/fine | Annualized European Union revenue drag | Likely outcome |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Investigation continues | 35% | $5m–$15m | 0%–0.5% | information, testing, and legal work; no final decision |
| Commitments/remediation | 45% | $20m–$50m | 0.5%–2.0% | age assurance, safer defaults, independent assurance, reporting changes |
| Moderate infringement | 16% | $50m–$125m | 2%–4% | formal finding plus meaningful sanction and product constraint |
| Severe tail | 4% | $175m–$350m | 5%–8% | unusually adverse finding or weak remediation; not the base case |

**Calculation:** probability weighting yields approximately **$30 million to $50 million** of incremental cash cost/fine and **$10 million to $20 million** of annualized revenue drag.

> `Central European Union value effect ≈ $30m–$50m + ($10m–$20m × 1.6)`
>
> `Central European Union value effect ≈ $46m–$82m, or $0.02–$0.04 per diluted share`

The proceeding could last beyond the 12-month valuation horizon. The model therefore assigns more weight to remediation than to a final fine.

## 4. United Kingdom: planned under-16 restriction is the new forward shock

### Current law versus proposed law

**Fact:** the Online Safety Act child-safety duties are already in force. Ofcom can investigate, require changes, and impose up to £18 million or 10% of qualifying worldwide revenue, whichever is greater. Business-disruption measures are possible for serious noncompliance. A statutory maximum is not a forecast. [Ofcom child-safety duties](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/protection-of-children-duties-under-the-online-safety-act) [Ofcom enforcement explanation](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/what-happens-when-a-company-doesnt-pay-a-fine)

**Fact:** Snap's Q2 filing says it notified United Kingdom regulators of planned tests and possible product changes designed to answer concerns about the effectiveness of safety measures. No public Snapchat-specific Ofcom enforcement decision was located by the cutoff, so this should not be called a concluded breach or fine. [Snap Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000052/snap-20260630.htm)

**Fact:** the United Kingdom government announced its intention to prohibit under-16 access to designated social-media services, explicitly including Snapchat. It plans regulations by the end of 2026, subject to affirmative approval in both Houses of Parliament, with implementation expected in spring 2027. This is a high-commitment policy plan, but it was not yet effective law at the cutoff. [United Kingdom government announcement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/social-media-to-be-banned-for-under-16s-in-landmark-government-move-to-givekids-their-childhood-back) [Government response, July 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/growing-up-in-the-online-world-a-national-consultation/outcome/growing-up-in-the-online-world-government-response-july-2026)

The intended package also includes:

- midnight-to-6 a.m. restrictions for ages 16–17;
- notifications muted and autoplay/personalized feeds off by default for ages 16–17, with an ability to change settings;
- broader limits on under-16 livestreaming and communication with strangers.

### Why Snap is disproportionately exposed

**Fact:** Snap's advertising tools indicated a United Kingdom ad reach of approximately 23.9 million people in early 2025. This is not DAU or monthly active users. [DataReportal United Kingdom 2025](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-united-kingdom)

**Fact:** Ofcom's passive-measurement panel found that United Kingdom Snapchat users aged 13–14 spent an average 2 hours 13 minutes per day in its 2025 sample. Among 8–14-year-old users, Snapchat averaged 841 visits per month. Samples and measurement coverage limit generalization, but the engagement signal is strong. [Ofcom Online Nation 2025](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/research-and-data/online-research/online-nation/2025/online-nations-report-2025.pdf?v=409414)

Snap's core friend graph requires logged-in accounts, whereas some competitors retain logged-out video viewing or have messaging products that may be treated differently. Meta is diversified across Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, and WhatsApp. TikTok and YouTube also face the regime, so some advertising damage is sector-wide. It is therefore reasonable to expect greater **percentage** user exposure for Snap, but unreasonable to assume all lost sector advertising flows back to Snap's adult inventory.

### United Kingdom scenario model

**Assumption:** United Kingdom revenue is 15%–25% of Snap's Europe region, or approximately $200 million to $330 million annually.

| Scenario | Probability | Timing | 12-month revenue effect through August 2027 | Incremental cost |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| On-time, broad scope | 60% | spring 2027 | $10m–$25m | $10m–$25m |
| Delayed or phased | 20% | later in 2027 | $3m–$10m | $8m–$20m |
| Materially softened | 15% | spring/later 2027 | $2m–$8m | $5m–$15m |
| Legal/political reversal | 5% | no near-term effect | ~$0m | $2m–$5m preparation |

**Calculation:** the probability-weighted next-12-month revenue loss is approximately **$8 million to $18 million**, with **$10 million to $20 million** of incremental implementation cost.

> `Central United Kingdom value effect ≈ $10m–$20m + ($8m–$18m × 1.6)`
>
> `Central United Kingdom value effect ≈ $23m–$49m, or $0.01–$0.03 per diluted share`

The **annualized** revenue loss after a full year of operation could be approximately $20 million to $50 million. Only part belongs inside the next-12-month cash flow because a spring 2027 start leaves roughly four to five months before the valuation date.

## 5. Aggregate regulation model and double-counting controls

### Gross central build

| Risk | Incremental cash cost/reserve | Annualized recurring revenue loss used for valuation | Value effect at 1.6 times revenue |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Australia, incremental from current run-rate | $5m–$10m | ~$2m | $8m–$13m |
| United States litigation + design rules | $120m–$150m | $20m–$35m | $152m–$206m |
| European Union Digital Services Act | $30m–$50m | $10m–$20m | $46m–$82m |
| United Kingdom under-16 plan | $10m–$20m | $8m–$18m within horizon | $23m–$49m |
| **Gross sum** | **$165m–$230m** | **$40m–$75m** | **$229m–$350m** |

### Required overlap haircut

**Assumption:** apply a 20%–30% haircut to recurring revenue effects and a 10%–20% haircut to shared implementation cost.

Why:

- the same minor cannot be lost twice within one country;
- age assurance, privacy-preserving verification, parent consent, notification controls, friend recommendations, and safety reporting use shared global systems;
- European youth-targeted-ad restrictions are already in historical results;
- Australia account locks are already mostly reflected in the first and second quarters of 2026;
- U.S. litigation remedies and state-law changes may require the same product work;
- existing legal and safety payroll is already in Snap's operating expense.

Do **not** haircut case settlements, fines, or region-specific revenue simply because shared engineering exists. Those cash outflows remain additive if incurred.

**Calculation:** at the central midpoints, the gross cash/reserve is about $198 million and the gross recurring-revenue effect is about $58 million. Applying a 15% shared-cost haircut and a 25% recurring-revenue haircut gives:

> `Central overlapped value ≈ ($198m × 85%) + ($58m × 1.6 × 75%)`
>
> `Central overlapped value ≈ $238m before subtracting any legal/safety expense already in the base model`

Using the scenario distribution rather than combining every range endpoint, and allowing for approximately $0 million to $40 million of cost already embedded in the operating forecast, the central module supports a rounded **$180 million to $260 million** probability-weighted value reserve, or approximately **$0.09 to $0.14 per diluted share**.

### Tail map—not the expected value

| Tail | 12-month probability | Approximate value effect | Main cause |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Manageable | 60% | <$250m | settlements remain case-specific; product controls largely shared |
| Material | 30% | $250m–$600m | U.S. settlement cycle, European commitments/fine, on-time United Kingdom restriction |
| Severe | 8% | $600m–$1.2bn | weak insurance plus broad U.S. remedy or unusually adverse European action |
| Extreme | 2% | >$1.2bn | multiple adverse regimes crystallize together; no current evidence makes this the base case |

The greatest non-cash risk is a lower valuation multiple: if investors conclude Snapchat's youth graph is structurally unmonetizable, the multiple can fall before revenue does. That sentiment effect belongs in scenario multiples, not as another cash deduction on top of this table.

## 6. Cost structure: is serving 493 million daily users becoming more efficient?

### Q2 2026: the reported gross-margin improvement is real

**Fact:** Q2 2026 revenue was $1.599 billion, up 18.9% year over year. GAAP cost of revenue was $667.9 million, up only 2.2%. GAAP gross profit was $931.1 million. [Snap Q2 2026 results](https://investor.snap.com/news/news-details/2026/Snap-Inc--Announces-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx)

> `GAAP gross margin = (revenue − GAAP cost of revenue) / revenue`
>
> `Q2 2026 = ($1,599.0m − $667.9m) / $1,599.0m = 58.2%`
>
> `Q2 2025 = ($1,344.0m − $653.3m) / $1,344.0m = 51.4%`

**Calculation:** the year-over-year increase is approximately 6.8 percentage points, or 680 basis points (bps). Cost of revenue rose about $14.6 million while revenue rose about $255.0 million, an unusually favorable incremental gross-margin quarter.

This is not purely a restructuring result: cost of revenue is dominated by cloud infrastructure, content/developer revenue share, subscription payment processing, and support/safety costs. The FTE reduction affects operating expense more directly.

### Gross-margin history

| Period | Revenue | GAAP cost of revenue | Calculated GAAP gross margin | Infrastructure cost |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2022 | $4.602bn | $1.815bn | 60.6% | $0.819bn |
| 2023 | $4.606bn | $2.114bn | 54.1% | $1.172bn |
| 2024 | $5.361bn | $2.474bn | 53.9% | $1.441bn |
| 2025 | $5.931bn | $2.670bn | 55.0% | $1.584bn |
| Q2 2026 | $1.599bn | $0.668bn | 58.2% | not separately disclosed for the quarter |

Sources: Snap [2024 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440825000019/snap-20241231.htm), [2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000013/snap-20251231.htm), and [Q2 2026 results](https://investor.snap.com/news/news-details/2026/Snap-Inc--Announces-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx).

The 2022–2025 record matters: margin first fell as infrastructure expanded, then began to recover. One strong quarter supports the improvement thesis but does not prove a permanently 60%-plus model.

### Infrastructure cost per user: stable, not collapsing

**Fact:** Snap guides 2026 infrastructure cost to $1.65 billion to $1.70 billion, $50 million above the prior range because of artificial-intelligence and machine-learning (AI/ML) investment intended to support revenue. It guides other cost of revenue excluding infrastructure to 16%–17% of revenue and targets 60%-plus gross margin. [Snap Q2 2026 prepared remarks](https://s25.q4cdn.com/442043304/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/Q2-2026-Prepared-Remarks.pdf) [April restructuring investor update](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000119312526155861/d36756dex992.htm)

**Assumption and calculation:** at $6.8 billion of 2026 revenue, the midpoints of those cost guides imply approximately 58.9% gross margin—not automatically 60%.

> `Implied gross margin = 1 − [$1.675bn / $6.8bn + 16.5%] = approximately 58.9%`

Holding the cost assumptions constant, revenue would need to be approximately $7.13 billion to reach 60%. Alternatively, infrastructure or other cost of revenue must land below the midpoints. The 60%-plus target is credible directionally after Q2, but it is not mathematically guaranteed by the guide.

**Fact:** Snap had $2.6 billion of non-cancelable commitments at June 30, 2026, primarily due within two years, covering hosting/data processing/storage as well as leases, content/developer partners, and other commitments. The amount is not all cloud infrastructure and is not debt, but it shows that a meaningful portion of the cost base cannot be switched off immediately. [Snap Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000052/snap-20260630.htm)

**Calculation:** using annual infrastructure cost and average or stated-quarter DAU:

> `Quarterly infrastructure cost per DAU = annual infrastructure cost / 4 / average DAU`

| Year | Infrastructure cost | DAU denominator | Quarterly infrastructure cost per DAU |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2022 | $818.6m | 354m average | $0.58 |
| 2023 | $1,172.0m | 400m average | $0.73 |
| 2024 | $1,441.1m | 438m average | $0.82 |
| 2025 | $1,584.1m | 470m average | $0.84 |
| 2026 guide midpoint | $1,675.0m | 493m Q2 snapshot | $0.85 |

The 2026 line is not perfectly comparable: it divides a full-year guide by one quarter's user snapshot. Snap's recent quarterly disclosures/inferences are approximately $0.82–$0.85 per DAU. The defensible conclusion is:

- **cash compute cost per user has not fallen versus 2022;**
- it has been broadly stable since 2024 despite larger recommendation and AI workloads;
- optimization is absorbing richer workloads rather than producing a visibly cheaper user;
- economic efficiency is improving because revenue per user and gross margin are rising faster than compute cost, not because compute is disappearing.

**Calculation:** at the 2026 infrastructure midpoint and 493 million Q2 DAU:

> `Annual infrastructure cost per DAU = $1.675bn / 493m = $3.40`

Q2 global average revenue per user (ARPU) was $3.25 for the quarter, or $13.00 annualized if Q2 were representative.

> `Infrastructure / annualized revenue per DAU = $3.40 / $13.00 = 26%`

At the 16.5% midpoint for other cost of revenue, a simplified annualized global unit bridge is:

| Per-DAU economics | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Annualized revenue | $13.00 |
| Infrastructure | ($3.40) |
| Other cost of revenue at 16.5% | ($2.15) |
| **Gross profit before operating expense** | **$7.45** |

This is **not** a regional profitability table. Rest of World quarterly ARPU was $1.00, but Snap does not disclose regional cloud, moderation, content, support, or marginal cost. A global average cannot prove that an Indian or Australian user is profitable or unprofitable.

### April 2026 restructuring: reconcile the claim

**Fact:** the restructuring affected approximately 1,000 team members, including a planned reduction of roughly 16% of FTE, and closed more than 300 open positions. Snap estimated $95 million to $130 million of restructuring charges, including $75 million to $100 million of future cash spending, and more than $500 million of annualized cost-base savings by H2 2026. [April 2026 Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000119312526155861/d36756d8k.htm) [Employee memo](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000119312526155861/d36756dex991.htm) [Investor update](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000119312526155861/d36756dex992.htm)

**Fact:** by June 30, Snap had recognized $128.5 million of restructuring charges: $74.4 million of severance and related charges, $26.5 million of stock-based compensation, and $27.6 million primarily for lease exits. Snap said most restructuring cash had been paid and both remaining charges and liabilities were immaterial. Therefore the April estimate of $75 million to $100 million of future cash spending should **not** be deducted again from a June 30-based valuation. [Snap Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, Note 14](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000052/snap-20260630.htm)

**Fact:** official FTE fell from 5,381 at March 31 to 4,723 at June 30, a decline of 658 or 12.2%. The gap from the 16% plan can reflect timing, local consultation, different start/end populations, and the approximately 1,000 figure including personnel other than FTE. [Snap Q2 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000156440826000052/snap-20260630.htm)

> `Sequential FTE decline = (4,723 / 5,381) − 1 = −12.2%`

**Fact:** management reduced its full-year adjusted operating-expense guide from about $3.0 billion to about $2.75 billion and its stock-based compensation (SBC) guide from about $1.2 billion to about $1.05 billion. These two changes explain about $400 million of annual guide reduction. Benefits, payroll tax, contractors, open roles, facilities, vendor spend, and timing can explain why the announced annualized run-rate exceeds that amount, but Snap has not published a complete bridge.

### Why more than $500 million is not more than $500 million of free cash flow

Savings can be consumed by:

- the $1.65 billion to $1.70 billion infrastructure guide;
- the cash portion of restructuring charges already paid during the transition, which affected first-half cash flow but is not a new June 30 deduction;
- safety, age assurance, legal defense, settlements, and insurance gaps;
- Specs development and launch spending inside the operating outlook;
- higher cash interest and eventual cash taxes;
- stock-based compensation differences that improve dilution economics but not current-period cash dollar for dollar;
- rehiring or contractor substitution.

The proof metric is **free cash flow per diluted share**, after legal/safety costs and without rebuilding FTE—not adjusted expense alone.

### Revenue per employee: strong snapshot, incomplete proof

| Snapshot | Revenue basis | FTE | Revenue per FTE |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2023 | $4.606bn annual | 5,289 year-end | $0.87m |
| 2024 | $5.361bn annual | 4,911 year-end | $1.09m |
| 2025 | $5.931bn annual | 5,261 year-end | $1.13m |
| Q2 2026 | $6.396bn annualized | 4,723 quarter-end | $1.35m |

> `Annualized Q2 revenue per FTE = ($1.599bn × 4) / 4,723 = $1.35m`

This is a directional productivity proxy, not a formal measure: annual revenue is divided by year-end headcount, Q2 is seasonal and annualized, and contractors are excluded. It nevertheless supports the claim that a smaller organization is carrying a larger revenue base.

### Artificial-intelligence efficiency: unusually concrete, still issuer-reported

Snap has disclosed more operational evidence than a generic “AI makes us efficient” story:

- **CodePal:** more than 200,000 code reviews in four months; coverage above 90% of pull requests (PRs); a reported roughly $0.40 cost per review; around ten minutes for a first review versus a cited five-hour human wait; thousands of issues identified; human approval remains required. [Snap Engineering CodePal](https://eng.snap.com/codepal)
- **Casper:** thousands of mergeable PRs per week using isolated environments, tests, search, automated review, and final human review. [Snap Engineering Casper](https://eng.snap.com/casper)
- **Internal support:** management said AI reduced internal support-ticket volume approximately 62% from the start of 2026.
- **Moderation:** first-pass image moderation automation increased from roughly 40% in Q2 2025 to nearly 90% in Q2 2026.
- **Development:** the restructuring update said more than 65% of new code was AI-generated, more than 7,500 bugs had been found through automated code review, and an internal agent answered more than one million support questions a month. [Snap Q2 2026 prepared remarks](https://s25.q4cdn.com/442043304/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/Q2-2026-Prepared-Remarks.pdf) [April restructuring investor update](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1564408/000119312526155861/d36756dex992.htm)

These data make higher throughput with fewer employees plausible. They do **not** prove that AI created the full $500 million saving. Management selected the metrics; reliability, rework, defect severity, contractor substitution, model-inference cost, and employee morale are not externally audited.

### Is the 493-million-user cost thesis now proved?

The precise answer is:

| Claim | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| “Serving each user is becoming much cheaper.” | **Not supported.** Infrastructure cost per DAU is broadly stable since 2024 and much higher than in 2022. |
| “Snap is serving more users without proportional compute inflation.” | **Supported since 2024.** DAU rose while cost per DAU stayed around the low-to-mid $0.80s per quarter. |
| “Economic efficiency is improving.” | **Supported currently.** Revenue grew 19% while cost of revenue grew about 2%; gross margin rose 680 bps. |
| “The $500m savings are fully realized.” | **Not yet.** They are an annualized H2 target; Q3 and Q4 are the proof period. |
| “AI caused the savings.” | **Plausible contributor, unproved magnitude.** Adoption metrics are concrete but self-reported. |
| “All Rest of World users are profitable.” | **Unknown.** Regional costs and marginal economics are not disclosed. |

### Cost downside scenarios

| Scenario | Probability | 2027 implication |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Durable operating leverage | 45% | gross margin around 59%–61%; FTE stays below ~4,900; infrastructure/DAU stable; most run-rate savings reach free cash flow |
| Partial savings | 40% | gross margin 56%–59%; legal, safety, Specs, and AI spend absorb 30%–60% of gross savings |
| Rebuild / quality problem | 15% | gross margin below 56%, infrastructure guide rises again, FTE/contractor base rebuilds, or product reliability weakens |

The cost-positive case can offset the central regulation reserve. More than $500 million of annualized savings is roughly twice the $180 million to $260 million probability-weighted regulation value reserve, but that comparison mixes annual expense, cash, and capitalized recurring revenue. It does **not** mean regulation is “free.”

## 7. What would change this view?

### Evidence that lowers regulatory risk

- eSafety closes its Snapchat investigation without material enforcement and shows falling underage account persistence;
- any October 2026 matter still involving Snap settles for evidently immaterial amounts or Snap obtains favorable rulings;
- insurance coverage is affirmed for major U.S. claim categories;
- the European Commission accepts commitments without a material fine or engagement/revenue impairment;
- United Kingdom regulations are delayed, softened, or create a genuine messaging exclusion that covers Snapchat's core use;
- age assurance produces low adult false-positive and abandonment rates.

### Evidence that raises regulatory risk

- a public Australian finding that 450,000 locks were not reasonable steps;
- an October bellwether verdict against Snap, if a Snap-specific matter proceeds, a large school-district settlement framework, or a weak coverage ruling;
- a U.S. injunction touching Streaks, friend notifications, or the private graph rather than only Spotlight;
- European interim measures, a statement of objections with major design restrictions, or a fine above $100 million;
- a broad United Kingdom implementation on schedule with no messaging carve-out and evidence of high 16–17 engagement loss;
- North America or Europe DAU falls faster after age-assurance deployment while adult verification failures rise.

### Cost scorecard for Q3 and Q4 2026

| Metric | Constructive | Warning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full-time employees | stays near or below 4,800 without service degradation | rapid rebuild toward >5,000 or contractor substitution |
| Infrastructure per DAU | approximately $0.82–$0.86 while ad/recommendation metrics improve | >$0.90 without matching revenue or ranking lift |
| Gross margin | reaches or exceeds 59%–60% | returns below 56% |
| Adjusted operating expense | tracks about $2.75bn annual guide | savings miss before new growth investment |
| Free cash flow per diluted share | grows after restructuring cash, safety, and legal costs | headline free cash flow grows but dilution/net debt worsens |
| Reliability | incident rate and advertiser delivery improve | AI output rises while outages, defects, or advertiser complaints rise |

## Final judgment

Regulation should be modeled as a **meaningful but bounded probability distribution**, not as either zero or an automatic global ban.

Australia demonstrates that governments can remove youth accounts and that Snap will fight, then comply. It also demonstrates why direct revenue arithmetic can overstate new valuation damage: the restriction began eight months before this memo, and the lost accounts are already largely inside current results. The United States has the largest cash tail because confidential settlements are accumulating and insurance is disputed, but the disclosed trial calendar and modest first external verdict do not justify assuming a tobacco-style master settlement. The European Union is more likely to produce commitments and product changes than a maximum fine within one year. The United Kingdom is the most important new cohort shock because its spring-2027 plan is explicit and Snapchat is youth-heavy.

On costs, the better conclusion is stronger than the old bear story but narrower than “AI made 500 million users cheap.” Snap is serving more users with broadly stable compute cost per user, monetizing those users better, and operating with 658 fewer official FTE than one quarter earlier. Q2 gross-margin expansion is hard evidence. The next test is whether the more-than-$500-million annualized saving survives legal/safety investment, AI infrastructure, Specs, and repeated-restructuring history—and becomes durable free cash flow per diluted share.
