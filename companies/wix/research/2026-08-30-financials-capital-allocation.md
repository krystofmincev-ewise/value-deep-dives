# Wix financials and capital allocation

## The economic shape

Wix has two reported revenue categories but at least four economic streams:

1. high-gross-margin recurring creation/hosting subscriptions;
2. professional and agency revenue sold through Partners/Wix Studio;
3. recurring business software such as bookings and CRM; and
4. lower-gross-margin payments and transaction revenue.

Base44 sits inside Creative Subscriptions and can also enter Partner metrics. Wix has one reportable accounting segment, so no audited Base44 SOTP exists.

Revenue compounded 11.9% from 2021–2025 and bookings 9.9%. Partner revenue became the fastest important channel, rising from $468.5 million in 2023 to $750.3 million in 2025, or 37.6% of revenue. That creates higher-value agency exposure but also concentrates the near-term risk in the channel whose bookings weakened in May–June 2026.

2025 Creative revenue was $1,409.7 million at an implied 83.2% GAAP gross margin. Business Solutions was $583.3 million at about 31.6%. Transaction revenue was $255 million, while recurring Business Solutions ARR was $312.4 million. Treating the entire Business Solutions line as high-multiple SaaS would be wrong.

H1 revenue was $1.104 billion and raw FCF $127.6 million, versus $290.1 million a year earlier. Management's adjusted H1 FCF of $173.4 million added back $37.3 million of acquisition cash costs and $8.5 million of restructuring cash costs. Those payments are real owner claims. The approximately $420 million full-year adjusted FCF guide is therefore not equivalent to normalized owner cash; $340–360 million is a more defensible raw 2026 placeholder until H2 reports.

Wix cut roughly 1,000 roles, about 20%, after Partner bookings weakened sharply in late May and early June. It expects $70 million of 2026 savings and $150 million at full run-rate. August guidance was low-to-mid-teens revenue growth, low-teens bookings growth and low-double-digit Q3 revenue growth. Applying 11%/13%/15% to 2025 produces $2.212/$2.252/$2.292 billion of 2026 revenue.

Base44's non-GAAP gross margin is expected near 60% in H2, versus near zero entering 2026. That is a major improvement, but still below Wix Creative's 80% Q2 GAAP margin, and Wix is reinvesting savings in Base44 acquisition and marketing.

## Revenue visibility is not the same as lock-in

At 2025 year-end, 83% of premium subscriptions were annual or multi-year. Deferred revenue was $854.3 million, RPO was $878.5 million and 83% of RPO was expected within twelve months. June 2026 deferred revenue was $911.6 million. Receivables and credit losses were well controlled.

These facts make the next few quarters visible. They do not disclose gross retention or prove that the full $1.96 billion ARR base is permanently locked. Premium subscriptions declined roughly 1% to 6.1 million in 2025 even as ARR grew 15%, showing that price/mix, Partners and Base44 are doing more of the work.

## Cash quality

The strongest bullish figure—2025's $573 million raw FCF—needs three adjustments in judgment:

- it included $237.4 million of SBC;
- deferred revenue increased $103.9 million; and
- accrued expenses and other liabilities increased $204.2 million.

The cash is real, but it is not identical to steady-state owner earnings. The model therefore uses owner FCF after an economic dilution charge, rather than both subtracting all SBC and separately assuming maximum dilution. Because raw FCF is after interest but before debt principal flows, the valuation adds normalized after-tax interest to produce FCFF, values enterprise cash flow and then applies one net-debt/claim bridge. Normalized cash taxes also need to rise: Wix paid only $3.6 million in 2025, benefited from a tax valuation-allowance reversal, and faces the 15% Pillar Two minimum from 2026. A 15–18% normalized cash tax is more credible.

## Base44 acquisition obligations

Wix acquired Base44 on 13 June 2025. Purchase-accounting consideration was $92.2 million: $18.1 million cash plus $74.1 million initial fair value of revenue-based contingent consideration. The liability rose to $89.5 million by year-end and became EY's critical audit matter. Founder and employees could also receive $43.0 million of cash retention/other payments through 2028 plus $8 million of equity awards.

A valuation cannot add back these cash costs and also assign Base44 a standalone value without an equity-bridge deduction. This dossier leaves them inside owner FCF and includes the last-audited contingent claim in net claims.

## Capital structure and the share-count trap

At 30 June 2026, Wix held $960.8 million of cash, deposits and marketable securities. It owed $500.1 million on a floating-rate credit facility and $1.15 billion face value of 0% convertible notes. Face-value net debt was about $689 million, before $410 million of operating lease liabilities. Some liquidity is pledged against the bank facility.

The share count moved as follows:

| Date / bridge | Shares m |
| --- | ---: |
| 31 Dec 2025 | 54.990 |
| 31 Jan 2026 | 55.047 |
| Pro forma after private placement | 58.314 |
| Shares tendered at $92 | (17.577) |
| Explicit company count, 11 May | 41.850 |
| Q2 weighted-average basic | 42.965 |
| Q2 non-GAAP diluted | 49.271 |

At $87.62, the basic equity value using 41.850 million shares is $3.67 billion. The Q2 non-GAAP diluted value is $4.32 billion. The public market-data feed's displayed $4.88 billion market capitalization implies roughly 55.6 million shares and has not incorporated the transaction correctly. The model's normalized pre-contingent denominator is 45.989 million: 41.850 million explicit basic shares plus 3.339 million unvested RSU/PSUs and a 0.8 million option/ESPP buffer. It then models modest issuance, treasury-stock warrant dilution above $104.73 and the convertible's debt/share switch above $210.49.

The convertibles initially convert at $210.49 and cover 5.464 million shares; capped calls run to $267.89. Warrants from the private placement cover up to 0.817 million shares at $104.73. At $87.62 both are out of the money. Every base enterprise-to-equity bridge subtracts the note principal and excludes conversion shares; modeled upside above the strike reverses the debt and includes dilution rather than double counting both.

## Buyback audit

From 2021–2025 Wix spent $1.600 billion to repurchase 12.154 million gross shares at an average $131.65. Yet year-end shares fell only 1.038 million from 2020 to 2025—1.9%. Roughly 91% of gross repurchases were offset by equity issuance.

| Year | Shares repurchased m | Cash spent | Average price |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2021 | 0.895 | $200.0m | $223.43 |
| 2022 | 2.773 | $231.9m | $83.62 |
| 2023 | 1.349 | $127.0m | $94.17 |
| 2024 | 3.594 | $466.3m | $129.75 |
| 2025 | 3.544 | $575.0m | $162.25 |

The March 2026 transaction finally caused a large net reduction, but it used cash and floating debt to tender at $92 after issuing 3.267 million shares to Durable Capital at $79.591 plus a warrant. From the January pre-placement count to May, the net decline was about 24%, not the gross 29.7% tender statistic.

Interpretation: management is willing to make a concentrated bet on the equity. That is confidence. But the previous prices, issuance offset and unusual financing mean it is weak evidence of valuation skill. Durable's participation adds outside validation, but it received a discount, warrant and lock-up.
