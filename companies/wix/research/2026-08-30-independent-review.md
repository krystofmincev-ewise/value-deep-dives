## Issues found and resolved

The first adversarial passes did not pass the dossier. They found that raw FCF had been treated as levered equity cash while enterprise-value methods also deducted debt; the dilution and convertible bridges were inconsistent; Base44 cost structure barely affected the then-voting valuation; structural regimes voted more than once; and the capital path used FCFF rather than levered FCFE. Evidence reviewers also found stale interview counts, unsupported workforce taxonomy phrasing, incomplete source provenance and filing-level bank-pricing/warrant caveats.

The passed version-4 snapshot:

- uses SOTP as the sole formal fair-value method; owner-FCFF DCF and cash multiple are non-voting cross-checks;
- converts levered cash to FCFF by adding normalized after-tax interest for enterprise valuation, but uses FCFE for retained-cash/debt-paydown sources and uses;
- separates the current $270m normalized owner-FCFF anchor, target core cash before future awards, the explicit $90–130m future-award charge and Base44 owner contribution;
- makes Base44 post-gross-profit cost load affect the voting Base44 SOTP multiple; a ±15-point load shift moves the twelve-month median to $136.53/$124.36;
- applies one claims bridge, treasury-stock warrant treatment and a conservative if-converted convention, while disclosing the omitted capped-call benefit and the 1.083m maximum warrant reserve;
- holds the formal horizon denominator flat after current awards; future-award economics are explicit in owner-cash methods and Base44's cost and retained-cash path, while the core SOTP multiple implicitly absorbs recurring awards;
- discloses that the structural regime deliberately bundles adoption/growth/multiple centroids while unit economics, WACC and draw-level shocks remain separate; and
- treats Partners as an overlapping channel rather than an additive segment.

The evidence review:

- confirmed that temporary complete transcripts were deleted and only timestamped original analysis remains;
