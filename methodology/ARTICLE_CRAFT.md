# Research articles that earn the reader's time

Use this workflow for a new investment article, research-backed explainer or
material body rewrite. A title change, factual correction or narrow edit should stay narrow. Follow the
user's audience, requested depth, budget and completion point; there is no
default word count or numerical editorial score.

The objective is an engaging, intelligible and complete answer to a worthwhile
question, with original insight earned by evidence. Readability must not remove
the research that makes the answer valuable. More research does not automatically
produce a better article: the writer must show what the evidence changes.

Use the [article evidence brief](../templates/article-evidence-brief.md) to connect
the research to the story. It can be a short working note, not another report.
Reuse an existing research plan when it already answers those questions; fill
only brief fields that affect the work. Keep verification and correction notes
in existing research or publication records where practical, rather than
maintaining duplicate forms.
Keep working briefs, before-copies and private feedback under ignored `.local/`.
The [research standards](RESEARCH_STANDARDS.md),
[valuation framework](VALUATION_FRAMEWORK.md) and
[publication workflow](SUBSTACK_PUBLISHING.md) retain their respective authority.

## Establish what the reader will gain

Before a full draft, state the question in ordinary language, the reader's
starting understanding and the answer the evidence currently supports. A topic
such as “AI and cloud economics” is not yet a question. “Can a successful AI
business still leave little cash for its shareholders?” creates something the
article can resolve.

For every central insight, connect:

**What is already known → the evidence or calculation we add → the mechanism it
reveals → what changes for the reader → what could overturn it.**

Originality can come from reconciling disclosures, testing a common claim,
connecting evidence that is usually considered separately, or making a hidden
assumption explicit. An unfamiliar metaphor, an unsupported forecast and a large
number of sources are not original findings. State whether the contribution is a
new observation, an author calculation, a conditional model result or a new
interpretation. Do not claim nobody else has noticed it without evidence.

Two examples from the repository illustrate the difference:

- The [Snap report](../companies/snap/thesis/2026-W34-final-report.md) does more
  than report a 10% advertising-price recovery. Its comparison of 100 → 90 → 99
  shows why a strong headline can largely restore an earlier decline. That
  changes which later evidence would establish durable improvement.
- The [Wix report](../companies/wix/thesis/2026-W35-final-report.md) distinguishes
  generating a page from moving the customer records, bookings and payments
  behind a working business. The insight is a difference in transition speed,
  with consequences for acquisition and retention; product names alone do not
  establish it.

These are editorial examples of the reports' reasoning, not fresh verification
of their dated investment conclusions.

If the proposed payoff rests on an unverified premise, investigate that premise
before polishing prose. If research supports only an ordinary conclusion, say so
and make it useful. Do not manufacture a surprising thesis to justify the work.

## Stabilize the analysis before polishing the article

Identify the canonical research, current model outputs, source cutoff and
valuation quantity. An article is an explanation of that work, not a second
valuation with independently chosen numbers. Where a generator produces the
article, identify its editable source; do not repair only a generated copy.

Trace each central quantitative claim to its inputs and calculation. Settle the
operating story, cash bridge and major countercase before spending time on a
finished opening. If a deeper cash-flow analysis changes the valuation, record a
research revision and rebuild the affected argument. Do not describe a changed
investment conclusion as copy editing or choose a multiple to preserve an
already-written ending.

Stabilizing the evidence is a working checkpoint, not a prohibition on learning.
When new evidence matters, update the canonical analysis and all dependent
article claims. Material changes invalidate the relevant prior review. A correct
old draft does not certify a changed model or a later article.

List the intended derivatives when research also feeds a published article,
interactive explainer or sequel. Check which conclusions each change affects and
carry the correction through every artifact within the authorized scope. New
research sitting only in an appendix or a later chat has not updated the earlier
article. Preserve publication history and use the existing update permissions;
do not silently resend a post.

## Build an argument before a section inventory

Write a rough sequence in which each step answers a question raised by the
preceding one. Use as many steps as the actual argument needs. For each proposed
section, name its reader question, strongest evidence, resulting inference and
consequence. A list of business, management, competition, valuation and risks is
a research checklist; it becomes a story when those sections explain one another.

Test the outline by retelling it without specialist terms. The writer should be
able to explain how the company earns money, what is changing, who benefits, why
that affects value and what remains unresolved. If the connection fails, repair
the reasoning or sequence before adding transitions and adjectives.

For an explainer, carry one concrete example through the difficult transition,
not only the easy first step. Show what stays fixed and what changes. For a
future scenario, explain the transition between dates: capability, demand,
supply, costs and competitive responses must support the next state. A year
label cannot explain a sudden shortage, surplus or profit. Scale a cost by the
activity that repeats; multiplying a one-time program by twelve is a model
assumption that needs a reason, not an explanation of annualization.

Reusing a familiar toy or example across experiments can conceal changed rules.
Announce a material assumption reset before its new result: identify the old
rule and its replacement. Calling it a separate experiment is not enough when
the reader is likely to carry the earlier rule forward.

The opening should establish the practical tension and an early useful finding.
It can begin with an observed fact, a concrete example or an authentic experience.
Do not invent a personal anecdote. Let the reader encounter the business before
asking them to absorb competing share denominators or model implementation.
Place the essential qualifier beside the finding; put formal conventions where
the reader first needs them.

Use headings that help a reader find the argument again. A long uninterrupted
block of paragraphs can be as hard to navigate as dozens of fragmented headings.
End by resolving the original question and explaining what would change the
answer. A sudden new company, mechanism or investment recommendation usually
needs its own development earlier or a separate piece.

## Make depth readable

Completeness means covering the evidence and objections needed to judge the
answer. For a requested exhaustive deep dive, retain all decision-relevant
business mechanisms, valuation drivers, risks and material unknowns. Do not turn
it into a short opinion piece merely to improve pacing.

Use the main article for the reasoning a reader needs to follow and challenge
the conclusion. Use linked supporting research for detailed source inventories,
full financial bridges, model specifications and alternative calculations. Keep
a material contradictory finding or an assumption that determines the verdict
in the main article even when the detailed work is linked. The article must make
sense without requiring readers to reconstruct it from the appendix.

Explain why a number matters before adding more numbers. A useful paragraph
often moves from a concrete fact through its economic mechanism to an inference.
For valuation, show the operating path that produces the cash and the mechanism
that turns that cash into today's value. A large near-term investment bill
followed by a trillion-dollar valuation needs an explanation of the recovery
already assumed; a reader cannot supply that bridge from the final number.

Teach a calculation once with a small worked example when it helps. Comparable
sections can then use the same convention and show only the meaningful
differences. Preserve exact reproducible calculations in the supporting record;
round article displays to match their uncertainty. Keep scenario narratives,
distribution percentiles, fair value and target-date market prices distinct.

Define a necessary term at first use, then use it consistently. In canonical
reports, follow the existing in-place plain-English-aside guidance. In an
article, a short explanation in the paragraph may be enough. Repeating an
acronym's full expansion or attaching a second explanation to an already-clear
sentence can obstruct understanding. A glossary cannot rescue a missing causal
explanation.

Uncertainty should tell the reader how the conclusion could change. Prefer a
specific dependency, counterexample or sensitivity to repeated lists of generic
caveats. Distinguish facts from assumptions at the point of use. Repeat a warning
when its meaning changes in a new context, not merely because another section
was written separately.

Use a chart, table or diagram when it makes the mechanism or comparison easier
to understand. Give it a clear quantity, period, units, evidence/assumption
labels and source. Verify the actual phone-width rendering. Decorative images,
dashboard thumbnails and tables of unrelated numbers do not establish depth.

## Diagnose the failure before revising

Read the complete current article. Preserve its strongest supported insights,
examples, voice and explanatory passages. Keep a local before-copy. Then identify
the smallest intervention that fixes the actual failure:

| Observed failure | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| The answer depends on an unsupported fact or an unexplained model choice | Reopen the specific evidence or calculation, then update affected claims. |
| Interesting research never changes the reader's interpretation | Develop the missing mechanism or test the proposed insight; extra adjectives will not supply it. |
| Good sections do not build toward one answer | Reorder or replace the outline, then integrate the whole piece. |
| The reader understands the business but cannot explain the valuation | Add the missing operating-to-cash or cash-to-value bridge from the existing analysis. |
| The payoff arrives after a long technical defense | Bring a supported finding forward; keep the qualification that changes its meaning. |
| Each new review makes the piece longer without adding understanding | Consolidate repeated explanations and decide which finding each section owns. |
| Simpler prose removes important research, a strong example or a real objection | Restore that substance and simplify its explanation. |
| A few sentences or labels obscure an otherwise sound argument | Make a focused line edit; preserve the structure and model. |
| Text is right but charts, links, tables or publishing state are wrong | Repair and inspect the rendered artifact or publication controls. |

Keep chat instructions, agent work logs, troubleshooting, old reviewer debates
and abandoned draft history out of reader-facing prose. Explain a substantive
change from a previously published conclusion when relevant. Explain missing
evidence and its effect; the tool that failed to retrieve it is usually not the
reader's concern.

One writer owns the integrated article. Delegate bounded evidence work or a
focused independent check when useful and allowed by the active task; do not
assemble the article by concatenating specialist memos. No standing reviewer
panel, automatic extra reviewer or open-ended rewrite loop is required.

## Review the complete result

Cold-read the finished article before reading its research notes or previous
review verdicts. A reader check should return a short retelling of the argument
and the exact places where understanding, trust or interest breaks. A simulated
reader is an editorial judgment, not measured audience evidence.

Then inspect the evidence behind the article's major claims, verify the displayed
calculations against the canonical outputs and check the strongest countercase.
Read the revised piece continuously after integrating changes: individually good
patches can create repetition, contradictory emphasis or a missing transition.

For a material finding, record the passage, what a reader would misunderstand,
why it affects the answer and the proposed repair. Separate required corrections
from optional preferences. If comparing drafts, name what improved and what was
lost; do not assume the latest version is better.

A useful acceptance check asks whether a reader can explain:

- the central question and what the research adds;
- the business mechanism and the evidence supporting it;
- how the important numbers follow from that mechanism;
- the strongest reason the conclusion might be wrong; and
- what evidence, price or condition would change the judgment.

The answer must come from the article, not from a reviewer's familiarity with
the project. Stop when the requested outcome is met and material findings are
resolved; cosmetic preferences do not justify another whole rewrite. If a
material gap remains at the user's budget limit, state it plainly and preserve
the usable work. Do not grant a higher score to declare completion.

“Ready for user review” identifies an editorial state. Source/model verification,
rendered publication checks and authorization to publish or send remain distinct.
Never infer publication or email permission from a successful editorial review.

## Learn from corrections without adding ceremony

For completed articles, retain a compact local record of the first delivered
draft, material user corrections, the accepted result and the reason for each
substantive revision. Classify the cause as evidence/model, missing insight,
structure/comprehension, lost depth/voice or publication mechanics. Count changed
decisions, not every autosave as a new version.

First distinguish a correction from a new scope request, a stylistic preference
or an enhancement to an accepted piece. Preserve explicit positive feedback and
its object: an accepted diagram or causal explanation is an asset to reuse, even
if another part still needs work. Silence is not acceptance, and later new
evidence does not by itself make the original work a failure. Use the compact
correction ledger in the brief instead of exporting full chat transcripts.

Use those records to test whether this workflow reduces repeated corrections
while preserving the user's desired depth and insight. Carry forward a specific
lesson only when the evidence supports it. Word counts, readability formulas,
agent scores and source counts can flag something to inspect; none proves an
article delivered value. Audience discovery and retention require their own
denominators and observation windows under the publication workflow.

For a prospective comparison, declare the included articles and first-delivery
point before evaluating results. Compare material correction types, repeated
corrections to the same problem and explicit human acceptance, with scope and
complexity beside each result. Do not claim this workflow has improved outcomes
until those articles provide evidence.
