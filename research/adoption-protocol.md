# Gradual AI adoption: experiment protocol

Started 11 October 2026, 20:37 UTC. User budget: three to four hours of active investigation; substantial local computation is separately permitted. Local research deliverables only. Existing ten-firm and banking results remain intact.

## Questions before results

1. Does spreading the same employment loss across periods alter the endpoint, the transition, or both?
2. Does automating a task reduce hours, pay, employment, prices, or some combination? Which conclusions depend on the contract?
3. When only one of two consumer businesses adopts AI, what happens to its rival and to total purchasing power?
4. When both adopt, does competition pass productivity gains through to consumers? Distinguish physical consumption, nominal GDP, real GDP, wages and leisure.
5. Who receives the AI bill? Consolidate the domestic supplier's receipts rather than treating them as a hole in GDP.
6. How do funded direct entry and acquisition differ from a service supplier, if a coherent implementation fits the investigation?
7. Which conclusions survive different demand, investment and distribution rules? What does seeded uncertainty change within each rule?

## Model choice

Retain the original ten-firm model for matched employment-ramp comparisons. Build a separate four-firm experiment: two competing consumer businesses, five workers each; one initially automated equipment producer; one initially automated AI provider. This makes relative prices and physical quantities explicit. Reusing the original monetary input-output coefficients with different prices would silently change physical technology. The new topology is a declared new toy economy, not a calibrated upgrade of the ten-firm network.

Same aggregate baseline: final consumption 80, investment 20, wages 60, gross surplus 40, nominal and base-price real GDP 100. Money stock 200. Equipment demand, capital and AI infrastructure assumptions must be disclosed. AI fees are intermediate purchases; acquisitions are financial transfers, not GDP. More automation is not itself evidence of more realized output.

## Sequence and acceptance

Run an early vertical slice, inspect its money flows, then change one main lever at a time. Test deterministic scenarios before adding randomness. Preserve paired random streams across comparable cases. Report Monte Carlo frequencies as conditional on chosen rules, never as real-world probabilities. Inspect physical-capacity constraints before interpreting output limits. No utility function means no claim to a welfare optimum.

Verify nonnegative balances, money conservation, wages/profits/value-added reconciliation, GDP from final expenditure, intermediate-fee consolidation, baseline stationarity, zero-adoption equivalence, symmetry, capacity and input bounds, reproducible seeds and funded transactions. Independent Sol and Astra reviews follow actual runnable results. Run proportionate tests and browser checks on the finished paper and explorer.

Write the short, illustrated story after the result set stabilizes. Keep the full protocol, model assumptions, case matrix, limitations and audit trail alongside it. Preserve the earlier article and clearly distinguish its monetary fixed-price model from this relative-price experiment.
