## Ranked actions and counterexamples

1. **Explain the cause of the cash-model failure precisely.** `saving_not_recycled` exhausts the original firm investment buffer, then capital depreciates. It stops after 18 periods with GDP 56.4859, contracted wages 60, cash wages 56.4859 and wage arrears 3.5141. It does not reach a valid zero-output equilibrium. **Counterexample:** run the same parameters with `depreciation_rate=0, periods=100`; GDP converges to 60, investment to zero, all wages are paid and there is no insolvency. The contrast separates failure to reinvest financial saving, failure to replace capital, and the final payroll failure. Preserve accrued wages versus cash wages in tables and captions; an insolvent final period is an explicitly terminated boundary, not a continuing debt-free economy.

2. **Carry the growth counterexample's technology and demand assumptions into the essays and chart captions.** The exact growth rate is `g=s*a−delta`. Positive consumption and sustained capital growth require `delta/a < s < 1`, so such a share exists only when `a>delta`. Default `a=.5, delta=.1, s=.3` gives 5% per invented period. **Counterexample:** `output_per_capital=.05, depreciation_rate=.1` shrinks capital even if every unit of output is invested; the default investment share gives −8.5%. The default positive path assumes automated replacement, no diminishing returns or scarce complementary inputs, willing owner consumption, and one flexible price that clears supply. It proves neither current AI feasibility nor unlimited physical growth in the actual world. `investment_share=1` grows GDP with exactly zero consumption, which prevents reading GDP as welfare.

3. **Limit the price conclusion to the experiment actually run.** The uniform price increase to 1.25 keeps nominal GDP at 100 and lowers real GDP to 80; uniform price .5 gives nominal GDP 100 and real GDP 200 when capacity permits. This does not estimate firms' relative prices, demand elasticities, markups, AI-service costs or cost pass-through. **Counterexample:** a single firm's higher price can transfer spending to it while another loses sales, which the common-price model cannot represent. Make nominal worker consumption and its price-deflated purchasing power visibly different: at price 1.25, 60 money units buy 48 units of output.

4. **Do not infer equilibrium scale from circular income identities.** At zero wages, investment zero and owner spending propensity one, `Y=C` leaves the scale indeterminate. It does not imply infinite or automatically positive GDP. The cash model selects a scale through inherited balances, lagged spending and payment rules. **Counterexample:** `no_initial_money` produces zero and stops with unpaid contracted wages; a zero-worker full-spending owner circuit can sustain positive spending with an existing money stock. The growth model separately stipulates constant nominal expenditure and clearing prices. Owner autonomous spending can recirculate indefinitely and should not be described as a necessarily depleted wealth buffer. The unemployed workers' initial cash and the no-equity firm buffer are the finite-buffer examples.

5. **Keep firm counts and group distribution within scope.** The production module splits each sector equally between two firms. It neither forces monopoly nor models competition, entry, strategic pricing or an adoption game. Replace “two competing firms” with “two firms” if that phrase survives. **Counterexample:** changing the number of identical firms while preserving sector totals changes no aggregate result. Workers also share one aggregate income/cash account; partial layoffs do not identify consumption of each fired individual, household inequality or welfare weights. Report worker-group versus owner-group consumption, and say that automated capacity is assumed rather than derived from layoffs.

6. **Document the full-depreciation growth boundary.** The accepted parameters `investment_share=0, depreciation_rate=1, periods=2` produce a zero-capital second period and raise `ValueError` because no positive price can clear positive nominal expenditure against zero supply. This is an economically meaningful exhaustion case rather than a malformed input. Either document that boundary explicitly or return a clear exhausted/infeasible status; it is not material to the four named growth cases.

## Independent execution evidence

Commands used the existing `python3` interpreter with `PYTHONPATH=studies/toy-economy/src`; no packages, browser sessions, external APIs or paid services were used. Small direct Python probes imported the public model functions and compared outputs with hand-derived identities.

- Baseline fixed-price GDP is 100. Layoffs of 1/3/5/10 give 94/82/70/40, with owner consumption 20 and investment 20 throughout. These results follow from `Y=W+2I` under the chosen propensities, not an estimated universal multiplier.
- The supply chain reports turnover 169.4, intermediate expenses 69.4 and GDP 100; workers and wage allocations sum to 10 and 60. Intermediate purchases are consolidated.
- All 3,168 explorer states were inspected programmatically: 1,703 equilibrium, 601 capacity-rationed, 76 infeasible-income, 128 indeterminate and 660 no-stationary-equilibrium. The 2,304 states with accounts passed independent expenditure/income/saving/consumption identities and household budget bounds. Rationing gives investment priority and forces consumption below planned spending; a rationed allocation is not satisfaction of the original consumption propensities.
- All 14 current named cash paths passed independent money conservation, nonnegative closing cash, contracted-wage income accounting, and `cash wages + arrears = contracted wages` checks. The 25-period baseline stays at GDP 100, consumption 80, investment 20 and capital 200. The added no-depreciation/no-equity-financing path reaches GDP 60.0000381 by period 24 with zero arrears and capital 300.
- Serialized current `build_results()` output matched saved `outputs/results.json` exactly after normalizing Python tuples to JSON arrays.
- Default automated GDP rises from 100 to 265.3298 by period 20, consumption from 70 to 185.7308, with nominal GDP fixed at 100 and wages zero. Replacement-only investment is stationary; no replacement depletes capital. The machines-only path has consumption zero despite rising GDP.

The calculations establish internal consistency for these examples. They do not establish parameter likelihood, a policy ranking, investment quality, observed AI effects or a forecast of a country's GDP. No universal wage-income floor for GDP follows from the identities; real feasibility and household access remain separate questions.
