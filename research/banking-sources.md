## B01 — Bank deposit creation and principal repayment

- **Title/authors/publisher:** *Money creation in the modern economy*, Michael McLeay, Amar Radia and Ryland Thomas; Bank of England, Quarterly Bulletin 2014 Q1.
- **Publication:** 2014-03-14. **Access:** 2026-10-11.
- **Canonical URL:** [publisher page](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2014/q1/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy); [official PDF](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/quarterly-bulletin/2014/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy.pdf).
- **Evidence type:** Central-bank explanatory research article. **Access/rights/retrieval/capture:** Public / link-only / web open, click, find / none.
- **Intended use:** Origination and principal-repayment entries; prudential and economic lending constraints; converting deposits to bank equity.
- **Verification:** PDF visual indices 2–3 (printed article pp. 16–17) on loan/deposit creation and repayment; visual indices 3–5 (printed pp. 17–19) on equity funding, profitability and risk/capital constraints.
- **Limitations:** Stylized UK banking explanation; does not validate the town's underwriting rules or experiment results. No quoted text or current money-share figures used.

## B02 — Practical limits and settlement outflows

- **Title/publisher:** *How is money created?*; Bank of England.
- **Publication:** Original publication date not shown; last updated **2019-10-01**. **Access:** 2026-10-11.
- **Canonical URL:** [Bank of England explainer](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/explainers/how-is-money-created).
- **Evidence type:** Official public educational explainer. **Access/rights/retrieval/capture:** Public / link-only / web open / none.
- **Intended use:** Explain why deposit creation is not unlimited lending and why payments to other banks create settlement needs.
- **Verification:** Read lending, repayment, capital/creditworthiness and interbank-payment sections on the page.
- **Limitations:** Simplified explanation. The one-bank model deliberately removes interbank withdrawals; do not turn that omission into a statement about real banks. Historical broad-money proportions are not used.

## B03 — Equity, losses and liquidity

- **Title/author/publisher:** *Bank regulation and supervision: from local to global to local*, Neil Esho; BIS/Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, lecture at ETH Zurich.
- **Publication/delivery:** **2025-05-27**. **Access:** 2026-10-11.
- **Canonical URL:** [publisher page](https://www.bis.org/speeches/20250527-bank-regulation-and-supervision-local-global-local); [official PDF](https://www.bis.org/speeches/20250527-bank-regulation-and-supervision-local-global-local.pdf).
- **Evidence type:** Primary supervisory educational lecture. **Access/rights/retrieval/capture:** Public / link-only / web open, click, find / none.
- **Intended use:** Bank equity as assets less liabilities; loan losses absorb equity; capital and liquidity are different concepts.
- **Verification:** Printed pp. 4–5 define equity and loan-write-off loss absorption; later liquidity discussion distinguishes the ability to meet withdrawals. PDF visual indices 5–6 contain the equity/write-off explanation.
- **Limitations:** A lecture, not a full regulation or resolution manual. The toy's 10% coefficient, if used, is an author assumption, not a regulatory requirement derived from this source.

## B04 — Financial claims and real resources

- **Title/authors/publisher:** *Banks are not intermediaries of loanable funds — facts, theory and evidence*, Zoltan Jakab and Michael Kumhof; Bank of England Staff Working Paper 761.
- **Publication:** Originally **2018-10-26**; accessed PDF revision **2019-06**. **Access:** 2026-10-11.
- **Canonical URL:** [official revised PDF](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/working-paper/2018/banks-are-not-intermediaries-of-loanable-funds-facts-theory-and-evidence).
- **Evidence type:** Central-bank-hosted authors' working paper. **Access/rights/retrieval/capture:** Public / link-only / web open, find / none.
- **Intended use:** Financing creates matching gross financial claims; deposits facilitate purchases of resources but are not themselves physical resources.
- **Verification:** Printed pp. 5–6 / PDF visual index 7, passage separating gross financial transactions from physical resources; first page verifies the revision and authors' policy disclaimer.
- **Limitations:** The authors' theory and modeling arguments do not represent an official Bank of England/IMF position. The note does not import their model's estimated responses or claim that all credit must cause additional investment.

## B06 — Debt-service flows and their limits

- **Title/publisher:** *Debt service ratios for the private non-financial sector — data documentation*; Bank for International Settlements.
- **Publication:** Original date not stated; document **last updated 2023-06-01**. **Access:** 2026-10-11.
- **Canonical URL:** [official methodology PDF](https://www.bis.org/statistics/dsr/dsr_doc.pdf).
- **Evidence type:** Official statistical methodology. **Access/rights/retrieval/capture:** Public / link-only / web open, find / none.
- **Intended use:** Distinguish outstanding debt from principal-plus-interest payments relative to income; clarify the roles of maturity and interest.
- **Verification:** pp. 1–2 define DSR, inputs and aggregate approximation error; p. 3 discusses income and FISIM adjustments. The actual document date, not the search engine's estimated date, is recorded.
- **Limitations:** BIS aggregate instalment approximations are not individual default cutoffs. The toy should use its own exact contractual payment schedule rather than treating BIS country ratios as a calibration.

## B07 — Affordability and the distribution of income

- **Publication:** **2022-09-07**. **Access:** 2026-10-11.
- **Canonical URL:** [official speech](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/speech/2022/september/colette-bowe-speech-at-the-2nd-research-workshop).
- **Evidence type:** Primary central-bank policymaker speech. **Access/rights/retrieval/capture:** Public / link-only / web open, find / none.
- **Intended use:** Assess repayment using income after essential spending and borrower-level vulnerability, rather than one aggregate debt/GDP rule.
- **Verification:** Sections on borrower/lender resilience and cost-of-living-adjusted DSRs; checked publication date on the page. Essential spending, interest, unemployment and distribution are discussed directly.
- **Limitations:** A historical UK mortgage setting, not a toy-firm underwriting specification. No historical ratio or numerical “danger threshold” is imported. No claim that this source proves debt never constrains GDP.

## B08 — Production versus financial claims

- **Title/publisher:** *NIPA Handbook, Chapter 2: Fundamental Concepts*; US Bureau of Economic Analysis.
- **Publication:** **Updated 2024-12**. **Access:** 2026-10-11.
- **Canonical URL:** [official chapter PDF](https://www.bea.gov/resources/methodologies/nipa-handbook/pdf/chapter-02.pdf).
- **Evidence type:** Official national-accounts methodology. **Access/rights/retrieval/capture:** Public / link-only / web open, find / none.
- **Intended use:** GDP measures production, while loans/deposits are financial claims; count newly produced investment, not loan principal, in GDP.
- **Verification:** Printed pp. 2-1–2-3 define production/financial assets; pp. 2-8–2-11 define GDP's expenditure, income and value-added approaches. Source also recorded in the earlier economics source log; reopened for this extension.
- **Limitations:** US accounting definitions, not a simulation or causal theory of credit. The loan-financing bookkeeping examples are original author specifications.

## B10 — Write-off is not necessarily debt forgiveness

- **Title/publisher:** *Your Money, Your Goals for legal aid organizations: Financial empowerment knowledge assessment for legal aid staff*; US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau.
- **Publication:** Exact date not printed in the inspected PDF; URL places the release in **2015-04**, recorded as an inferred release month, not a verified exact date. **Access:** 2026-10-11.
- **Canonical URL:** [official educational PDF](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/201504_cfpb_ymyg_knowledge-assessment-quiz_legal-aid.pdf).
- **Evidence type:** Official public educational answer key. **Access/rights/retrieval/capture:** Public / link-only / web open, find / none.
- **Intended use:** Keep accounting charge-off distinct from cancellation of a borrower's legal obligation.
- **Verification:** Question 23 and answer, printed pp. 17–18: charge-off does not by itself prevent continued debt collection.
- **Limitations:** Historical US consumer-debt example, not legal advice or a statement of every jurisdiction's insolvency law. Toy default/forgiveness must be explicitly defined. No unrelated numerical or legal claims are used.
