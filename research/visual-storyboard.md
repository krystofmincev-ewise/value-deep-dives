# Visual teaching edition — 11 October 2026

User objective: a teenager should follow who receives money, who spends it and
what changes without carrying an invisible ledger between paragraphs. Preserve
the car → owner → customers → investment → car narrative. Build and inspect static
figures first; only then create the animated reading edition from the same figures.

## Visual grammar

Workers / former workers: green, always explicitly named. Owners: gold.
New equipment: blue. Unpaid obligations or lost orders: rust. A number denotes
an amount only when its label supplies the quantity and units. Household groups
remain present after wage income disappears. Icons are illustrations, not scaled
counts, unless a count is explicitly stated. Final-purchase bars share scales
within a comparison. Money stock and income/spending flows are never added.

## Story and figure sequence

1. Meet the town: five pairs of businesses, ten workers, ten owners.
2. Income: one output total100 becomes wages60 plus owner income40.
3. Spending: worker60 goes to household purchases; owner40 divides20/20.
   The same100 returns as household purchases80 plus equipment20. Finance is not
   counted as another output purchase.
4. Private incentive: one firm saves6, bears1 of10 lost sales, gains5 before setup.
   Separate equal-share game, not an optimizing version of the cash ledger.
5. No wages, owner consumption80 and equipment20: output100, former-workerC0.
6. Shared ownership: distribution60/40 restores the initial spending pattern,
   with dividends replacing wages. Owners consume half their40; do not imply
   ownership is the only parameter changed from the prior owner-MPC.8 case.
7. Smaller town: fixed equipment20 and ownerC20 support output40. Compare100.
8. Contracting purchases: income100 yields next purchases50+20=70, then35+14=49,
   then24.5+9.8=34.3. Cash stock200 remains separate from these flows.
9. Payday: each capital firm has1 for wages3; gap2 each. AggregateGDP67.75 is not
   available cash in the failing firm's account.
10. Survives at lower GDP: after freeze15, workerC60/ownerC0/I20 giveY80. Separate
    from failure case9; reset scenario clearly in prose.
11. Replacement versus expansion: equipment replaces worn capacity or adds to it.
    Conceptual diagram, not another numerical simulation.
12. What is output for? Fixed diminishing-return technology: Y250=C125+I125 versus
    Y500=C0+I500; both stationary, investment equals replacement.

## Motion contract (after static acceptance by inspection)

Same article, same figures, same figures' values. Each player has numbered steps,
previous/next, play/pause and reset. Motion traces the labeled direction of a payment or causal relationship; it does not imply exact payment timing or forecast time. Only one
player may run at once. No autoplay on page load or scroll. Stop offscreen/hidden.
Reduced-motion mode uses instant steps. Print shows the complete static diagram.
All stages and captions remain understandable without JavaScript. No remote code,
telemetry, live model calls or new economics in the animation.
