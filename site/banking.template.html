Start both towns with the same money

Before this experiment, the owners put 20 of their firms’ cash into the bank as its loss-absorbing capital. Worker accounts hold 60, owner accounts hold 40 and firm accounts hold 80. That leaves 180 in customer accounts and 20 of bank capital, backed by the original 200 of base cash.

The bank exists in the comparison without lending too. Neither version receives a gift of new wealth. A loan can subsequently create a new spendable deposit, but the borrower owes an equal amount.

One bank; no fees, withdrawals, bank runs or outside rescue. The bank’s operating costs and service production are omitted. Rates are per invented round, not annual rates. The earlier paper’s 100 of firm buffers is now 80: compare the matched controls here.

Rounds are numbered from zero. The slider shows existing Python results; it does not run a second economic model in the browser. There is no autoplay.

Buying from another account holder moves a deposit; it does not change the total. New loans increase that total. Principal repayments reduce it. Interest stays out until the bank pays it back into customer accounts. A forgiven loan reduces bank capital; it does not erase the deposits already created in the town.
