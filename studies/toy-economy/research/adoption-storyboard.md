# The town adopts AI one business at a time

Working story plan, before final numbers. The finished article should be shorter than the prior illustrated paper while retaining frequent pictures. Numbers below are definitions or baseline accounts, not unreviewed simulation claims.

1. **Return to the original question.** AI needs a customer. A sudden disappearance of wages isolates a mechanism; now watch the transition. Picture: the same starting money loop, with two shops, ten workers, owners, an equipment maker and an AI supplier. One main question: where do the productivity gains go?
2. **A task disappears before a person does.** Ten full shifts make eighty baskets. If AI does 40% of the tasks, the same baskets need six human shifts. Picture: ten shift blocks, four transferred to AI. Three doors: keep pay and use the time; keep pay and expand; reduce paid work. Explain person, contracted time and actual productive time separately.
3. **One shop goes first.** Follow its price, customers and rival. Picture: A/B side-by-side at the same round, spending arrows, finite capacity and remaining cash. A cheaper shop can win business without the town becoming richer. Do not infer optimizing competition from an imposed price rule.
4. **Then the rival catches up.** Both gain the technology; what happens now depends on pay, spending and price pass-through. Picture: a small set of matched outcomes using baskets bought by worker households, paid work and GDP. No dense table in the reading path.
5. **The AI bill has a destination.** Consumer firms pay the supplier; it distributes or retains income according to the model. Picture: a payment that moves to owners or workers. Intermediate fees are counted once, not added again to GDP. Acquisition is a transfer; entry requires newly produced equipment.
6. **Speed and uncertainty.** Show sudden versus steps from one starting point, then one actual seeded path. Distinguish a softer transition from a different endpoint. Picture: clear line chart and visible stopped runs. Quantiles are outcomes under invented timing rules.
7. **What breaks, and what rescues it?** Order short causal chains: income loss→less spending; rival loses sales→cash buffer runs down; constraints→unfilled purchases. Distinguish a recorded payroll failure from an assertion that all future GDP becomes zero. Show sharing/price/capacity counterexamples and the earlier banking bridge.
8. **Return to the customer.** Technology opens possibilities; contracts, prices, ownership and spending decide who can use them. Tie directly to the original GDP paper, acknowledge where the stronger formulation “no wages means no growth” was too broad. No AI revenue/capex forecast is validated by this toy town.

Static pictures first. Matching optional step-by-step edition may reuse the already-tested reader controls. Every figure's numbers must come from saved Python records; no independent economic calculations in browser code. Advanced assumptions, complete sensitivity grid and audit trail live beside the short paper, not as interruptions to its story.
