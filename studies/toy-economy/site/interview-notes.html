Our analysis: the question is not whether company revenue must be paid from newly created GDP. Existing wages, other supplier spending and profits can be redirected; new capital purchases can also create demand. The stricter question is whether customers and investors repeatedly finance those purchases at prices that cover the full economic costs. Revenue is neither profit nor free cash flow.

Capital financing episode

SemiAnalysis Weekly episode 037: Who's Funding the $11 Trillion AI Buildout?, Daniel Nishball and Jordan Nanos, published 9 October 2026, duration 1:23:17.

At 6:37–7:41, the speakers identify $11.3 trillion as cumulative 2024–2029 AI IT and data-center capex, broader than hyperscaler spending. This is not $11.3 trillion in one year. The selected transcript, 6:56 playback position and corresponding speaker frame were checked. Their expenditure forecast was not independently rebuilt.

It supports separating the construction bill, financing commitments and subsequent operating demand. A lender's claim is not a new final good. Spending the borrowed money on a newly produced machine can be investment; servicing the loan later is a distinct flow.

SemiAnalysis papers

AI Dark Output: The Visible Cost of Invisible Output, Malcolm Spittler and Dylan Patel, 29 May 2026, was readable in the authorized Chrome session. Its useful challenge is that cheap AI services may create benefits which output and price statistics capture imperfectly. No proprietary charts or task-exposure data are reproduced.

Our accounting qualification: moving a purchased business input in-house does not by itself reduce aggregate GDP by the avoided invoice. If final sales remain unchanged, value added can move from the supplier to the adopting firm. A move from a purchased household service to unpaid household production is a different production-boundary problem. Separately, a mismatched service-price index can distort measured real growth. The toy model measures a known homogeneous quantity and therefore does not estimate the empirical size of such measurement errors.

What remains unresolved

Exact audio units and annual-versus-run-rate basis in the Big Technology forecast; comparable geography and double-counting boundaries for the $2 trillion capex total; customer willingness to pay, deployment, margins and actual financing. None of these is resolved by a toy economy. The simulation tests economic possibility and mechanisms, not whether the forecasts will occur.
